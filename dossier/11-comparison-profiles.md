## 'Oumuamua

- **designation:** 1I/2017 U1
- **discovered:** 2017-10-19, Pan-STARRS1 (Haleakala, Hawaii) — found ~40 days after perihelion, on the way out
- **eccentricity:** 1.20113
- **inclination_deg:** 122.74
- **perihelion_au:** 0.2559
- **perihelion_date:** 2017-09-09
- **closest_earth_au:** 0.1618
- **v_infinity_kms:** 26.33
- **size_estimate:** ~100-400 m long (est. 100-1,000 m range), width/thickness ~35-167 m; extreme axis ratio ~6:1 to 10:1 (cigar or pancake shape)
- **nature:** Inert/rocky-appearing; no detected coma, dust, or gas despite deep searches — yet accelerated like a comet. Composition still debated (nitrogen ice, hydrogen ice, dark organic-rich crust all proposed)
- **weird_notes:** ['Non-gravitational acceleration (~5x10^-6 m/s^2 at 1 AU, radial ~2.8x10^-7 AU/d^2) with NO visible outgassing — no coma, no dust, no detected gas to explain it', 'Extreme lightcurve amplitude ~2.5 magnitudes implying axis ratio of at least 6:1, more elongated than any known solar-system asteroid or comet', 'Tumbling (non-principal-axis) rotation, period ~7.3-8 h', 'Red, comet-like surface color but zero cometary activity detected by Spitzer and deep ground-based imaging', "Excess velocity vector close to the Local Standard of Rest — dynamically 'young' / unusual for an ejected planetesimal", 'Only detected post-perihelion at close range; observed arc was short (~2.5 months), so many properties remain unresolved']
- **loeb_position:** Loeb (Bialy & Loeb 2018, ApJL) argued the non-gravitational acceleration without outgassing could be explained by solar radiation pressure on a thin, flat artificial object — a lightsail or discarded 'technological debris' from an alien civilization. He expanded this in his 2021 book 'Extraterrestrial,' maintaining the artificial-origin hypothesis is more plausible than exotic natural explanations like hydrogen icebergs.

- **eccentricity:** 3.356
- **inclination_deg:** 44.05
- **perihelion_au:** 2.007
- **perihelion_date:** 2019-12-08
- **closest_earth_au:** 1.9
- **v_infinity_kms:** 32.2
- **size_estimate:** Nucleus effective radius 0.2-0.5 km (HST); early ~975 m estimates were revised down. Coma/tail much larger (tail ~160,000 km)
- **nature:** Clearly cometary from first detection — bright coma and tail, normal dust and gas production. The 'ordinary' interstellar object; behaved like a moderately active solar-system comet
- **weird_notes:** ['Extremely CO-rich: CO/H2O ratio 3x+ higher than any comet measured in the inner solar system, suggesting formation beyond the CO ice line of a cold M-dwarf or outer disk (Bodewits et al. / Cordiner et al. 2020)', 'NH2-rich chemistry relative to typical comets', 'Unusually pristine dust — suggested it had never before passed close to any star', 'Nucleus fragmented/split in March 2020 after an outburst, post-perihelion (a normal thing for comets to do, but notable)', 'Otherwise the control case: proved interstellar comets can look exactly like ordinary comets']

He noted its pristine condition was mildly interesting but did not claim artificiality.
