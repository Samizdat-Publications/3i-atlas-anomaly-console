### O-01 — Origin at the Local Standard of Rest (the 'buoy at rest')

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2017-10-31
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Before entering the solar system, 'Oumuamua was moving at only ~10 km/s relative to the Local Standard of Rest (LSR): its heliocentric velocity was (U,V,W) = (-11.5, -22.4, -7.7) km/s (26.32 km/s total), which in the LSR frame leaves negligible radial (U) and vertical (W) galactic motion and only a ~11 km/s lag in the rotational (V) component. Mamajek (arXiv:1710.11364, submitted Oct 31, 2017) computed that its velocity sat within 5 km/s of the median UVW velocity of local field stars and that fewer than ~1 in 500 field stars in the solar neighborhood have velocities so close to that local median (essentially the LSR). The Sun itself moves faster relative to the LSR (~18 km/s peculiar motion) than 'Oumuamua did.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb argued this is exactly where you would park an artificial object meant to survey passing stars — or to hide its origin: in 'Six Strange Facts about 'Oumuamua' (arXiv:1811.08832, Nov 2018) he noted only one star in 500 moves as slowly in the LSR frame and called the LSR 'the ideal frame for camouflage,' avoiding association with any particular star. In 'Extraterrestrial' (2021) he counted the ~1-in-500 kinematic coincidence among the improbabilities any natural ejection model must explain, since ejected rocks inherit their parent star's velocity plus an ejection kick — 'Oumuamua was effectively at rest in the galactic frame and the solar system ran into it.

> Perhaps 'Oumuamua was like a buoy resting in the expanse of the universe, and our solar system was like a ship that ran into it at high speed.
>
> — Avi Loeb, 'Extraterrestrial' (2021) — verbatim; same buoy-and-ship analogy repeated in 2019-2021 interviews. The LSR/1-in-500 'camouflage' argument is in 'Six Strange Facts about 'Oumuamua' (arXiv:1811.08832; Scientific American, Nov 20, 2018).

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Young objects ejected at low velocity from nearby young stellar systems naturally cluster near the LSR before gravitational scattering ('disk heating') ages their kinematics; the low LSR velocity implies 'Oumuamua is dynamically young (<~100 Myr — Hallatt & Wiegert 2020, arXiv:1911.02473, AJ; Hsieh, Laughlin & Arce 2021 linked it to the ~30 Myr Carina moving group and a molecular-cloud origin, arXiv:2105.14670, ApJ). The ISSI team (Nature Astronomy 3, 594, 2019) treated the kinematics as pointing to a young planetesimal population, not design.

### O-02 — Extreme lightcurve elongation (~10x brightness swings)

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2017-11-20
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Meech et al. (Nature 552, 378; published 2017-11-20 with ESO release eso1737) reported brightness variations by a factor of ~10 (about 2.5 magnitudes) every ~7.3 hours as the object rotated. The original fit implied an axis ratio of up to ~10:1; later reanalyses accounting for phase-angle effects put the most likely aspect ratio at ~6:1 (6±1) — still more elongated than any of the hundreds of thousands of asteroids and comets ever measured in the solar system (typical bodies are ~2:1 or 3:1).

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb repeatedly listed the record-setting elongation as strange fact number one: no known solar-system body comes close, and an extreme aspect ratio is what an artificial thin structure (cigar or disc seen edge-on) would produce. In 'Six Strange Facts' (arXiv:1811.08832, also published in Scientific American on 2018-11-20) he noted the shape inference, combined with the later acceleration anomaly, pushed him toward a thin-membrane geometry rather than a rocky ellipsoid.

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Natural shaping mechanisms proposed: tidal fragmentation of a body during a close stellar or planetary encounter can spin off highly elongated splinters (Zhang & Lin, Nature Astronomy, April 2020, reproduced ~10:1 and even more elongated fragments); prolonged interstellar-dust erosion or collisional grinding can also elongate small bodies. The ISSI team (Nature Astronomy 3, 594, 2019) called the shape unusual but consistent with a purely natural origin, noting that lightcurve amplitude is enhanced at the observed phase angles (19-27 deg), softening the required ratio to ~6:1; McNeill et al. (2018) independently derived 6±1 from lightcurve inversion, and Vazan & Sari (MNRAS 493, 1546, 2020) argued irregular surface reflection properties could mimic part of the amplitude, allowing an even less elongated body.

### O-03 — Complex tumbling (non-principal-axis rotation)

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2017-12-01
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Photometry from multiple telescopes could not be phased to a single rotation period; Fraser et al. (Nature Astronomy, arXiv 2017-11-30, published 2018-02-09) and Drahus et al. (Nature Astronomy 2018, Gemini North data) showed 'Oumuamua is tumbling — rotating in an excited non-principal-axis state with lightcurve periodicities near ~7.3-8.7 h (Drahus: ~7.34 h average; Belton: 8.67 h). Belton et al. (ApJL 856, L21, April 2018) formalized the spin-state families from 818 observations over 29.3 days. Fraser et al. computed a damping timescale for the tumbling of at least a billion years, so it likely tumbled through interstellar space since a violent event (e.g. a collision) long before reaching us.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb counted the excited spin as another oddity constraining natural origins (a collision or fragmentation is needed to set it tumbling) and argued the tumbling made the outgassing explanation for the acceleration harder: citing Rafikov (2018), he noted that cometary jets should have torqued the object and rapidly changed its spin — Rafikov showed outgassing strong enough to supply the acceleration would drive spin evolution within days (even rotational breakup) unless the jet's torque lever arm was finely tuned, yet no spin change or sudden kicks were observed. Separately, Loeb cited Mashchenko's (2019) lightcurve-torque modeling approvingly for its conclusion that the shape is disc-like at ~91% confidence — though Mashchenko's own best fit (a thin disc under moderate outgassing torque of comet-like amplitude) actually supports the natural-comet reading.

> showing no change in spin or sudden kicks as routinely observed from localized jets
>
> — Loeb, 'On the Possibility of an Artificial Origin for `Oumuamua', Astrobiology review (lweb.cfa.harvard.edu/~loeb/Loeb_Astrobiology.pdf), citing Rafikov 2018

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Tumbling is common among small solar-system bodies after collisions; Fraser et al. attributed it to a collision in 'Oumuamua's birth system before or during ejection. Belton et al. showed several spin states fit the data, including ones consistent with either cigar or pancake shapes. Rafikov (2018) argued the observed spin stability limits any outgassing torques — a point against the comet interpretation — but later modeling (Mashchenko 2019; Seligman et al.) showed outgassing concentrated at the substellar point can supply the acceleration while leaving the tumbling period essentially unchanged.

**Verification notes:** Core facts all verified (date within window; Fraser arXiv 2017-11-30 / Nature Astronomy 2018-02-09; Drahus ~7.34 h; Belton ApJL 856 L21 April 2018, DOI aab370; billion-year damping timescale is Fraser et al.'s own statement; all four source URLs resolve to the claimed papers, with the fourth being Mashchenko 2019 MNRAS 489, 3003, arXiv:1906.03696). Two fixes: (1) loeb_take conflated three claims — the 'very finely tuned outgassing' argument belongs to Rafikov (2018: fine tuning of the jet's torque lever arm needed to avoid rapid spin evolution 'on a timescale of a few days'), not to Mashchenko, whose own 'fine-tuning problem' wording refers to cigar-shaped models (16% probability) vs disc models (91%); Loeb cited Mashchenko approvingly for the ~90%-confidence disc-like shape, and Mashchenko's fitted torque was actually comet-like in amplitude (supporting the natural interpretation). Reworded loeb_take accordingly. (2) official_explanation's 'used both for and against' clause sharpened: Rafikov's spin-stability point argued against the outgassing interpretation; later substellar-point outgassing models (Seligman et al.) reconciled stable spin with jets. Also added a verified short Loeb quote (extracted directly from his Astrobiology review PDF) to the previously empty loeb_quote field, and added the Rafikov ApJL DOI link to sources.

### O-04 — Breakthrough Listen radio silence

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2017-12-13
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

On 2017-12-13 (3:45-9:45 pm ET) Breakthrough Listen observed 'Oumuamua with the Green Bank Telescope across four bands (L, S, X, C; 1.1-11.6 GHz), accumulating ~90 TB of raw data over the 2-hour on-source observation plus calibration scans. No artificial narrowband signals were found. The published analysis (Enriquez et al. 2018, Res. Notes AAS 2, 9) set limits sensitive to omnidirectional transmitters with power as low as ~0.08 W — a fraction of a cellphone — at the object's distance.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb (who chairs Breakthrough Starshot's advisory committee) endorsed the search and treated the null result as consistent with, not fatal to, the artifact hypothesis: a defunct piece of technological debris or a passive lightsail need not transmit. In 'Extraterrestrial' he argued most artifacts we encounter would likely be dead 'space junk' of other civilizations, so radio silence carries limited evidential weight.

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

The mainstream reading is straightforward: a natural rock does not transmit, and the deep non-detection removed the most testable technosignature prediction. The SETI Institute's Allen Telescope Array had already observed it 2017-11-23 to 12-05 (Harp et al. 2019, Acta Astronautica) and independently found no signals down to ~10 W EIRP at 1 GHz (rising to ~100 W at 7 GHz), and the ISSI team's Nature Astronomy review found no compelling evidence for an artificial origin, citing the radio quiet as consistent with a natural body.

### O-05 — Population density too high for ejected rocks

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2018-03-06
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Scaling from Pan-STARRS's single detection, Do, Tucker & Tonry (ApJL 855, L10, published 2018-03-06) inferred a space density of 'Oumuamua-like objects of ~0.2 per AU^3 — a mass density of ~4 Earth masses of ejecta per cubic parsec, requiring each star to account for over 10^16 ejected bodies. The required planetesimal disk mass exceeds all estimates of the solar system's own planetesimal disk by an order of magnitude; Loeb noted the density is 2-8 orders of magnitude above the pre-detection prediction of Moro-Martin, Turner & Loeb (2009), and rounded the requirement to ~10^15 (a quadrillion) objects per star.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb ('Six Strange Facts', arXiv:1811.08832, and book 'Extraterrestrial') argued each star would need to eject ~10^15 'Oumuamua-like objects — 2-8 orders of magnitude more than he and Moro-Martin had predicted in 2009 — an implausible budget if they are random rocks. A random population of 10^15 solar sails per star is equally unreasonable, so he argued targeted trajectories 'add explanatory power': far fewer artifacts are needed if they aim for the habitable zones of stars rather than sampling all orbits randomly. Separately, Siraj & Loeb (New Astronomy 2022) ran the same mass-budget attack against the nitrogen-iceberg model, and Loeb repeatedly cited Moro-Martin's 2018-2019 finding that ejection scenarios fall short.

> Each star in the Milky-Way needs to eject 10^15 such objects during its lifetime to account for the inferred population, much more than anticipated based on the Solar System.
>
> — Loeb, 'Six Strange Facts about 'Oumuamua', arXiv:1811.08832 (Nov 2018; also Scientific American, 2018-11-20)

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

The ISSI team (Bannister et al., Nature Astronomy 2019) countered that with a single detected object there are no firm constraints on the size-frequency distribution, so the measured number density cannot be converted to a mass density: plausible SFDs with a few percent of mass in ~50 m fragments match the observed density with only 0.02-15 Earth masses ejected per star, making the 'higher than expected' claim unsupported. Do et al. themselves proposed Oort-cloud shedding by post-main-sequence stars as a supplementary source; Zhang & Lin (2020) and Desch & Jackson (2021) each predict abundant small fragments. Moro-Martin (2018, 2019), however, found the tension persists under many assumptions — the debate hinged on the unknown size distribution, which detections of 2I and 3I are only now starting to constrain (km-scale ISOs are far rarer, ~10^-4 to 10^-2 per AU^3).

**Verification notes:** Date 2018-03-06 CONFIRMED exact (ApJL publication date). Density 0.2 au^-3 and ~4 M-earth/pc^3 confirmed verbatim. THREE corrections: (1) Do et al. state 'each star must account for over 10^16 ejected bodies', not ~10^15 — the quadrillion-per-star figure is Loeb's rounding in Six Strange Facts, now attributed correctly; Do et al.'s own stated excess is 'an order of magnitude' over solar-system planetesimal-disk estimates, while the '2-8 orders of magnitude above prediction' comparison is Loeb's, versus Moro-Martin, Turner & LOEB 2009 (Loeb co-authored the prediction — 'Moro-Martin's own tension' framing fixed). (2) Official explanation misattributed: Moro-Martin 2018/2019 CONFIRMED the tension rather than resolving it; the actual rebuttal is the ISSI team's size-frequency-distribution argument ('with a single detected object there are no firm constraints... the claim that the observed number density is higher than expected... is not supported'), not primarily Poisson error; 'later surveys refining density downward' softened to an SFD-constraint statement since 3I-era densities (~10^-4-10^-2 au^-3) apply to km-scale objects. ### O-06 — Non-gravitational acceleration with no visible coma or tail

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2018-06-27
- **Verification:** `CONFIRMED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Micheli et al. (Nature 559, 223; published 2018-06-27) detected, at ~30-sigma significance, a non-gravitational acceleration of 'Oumuamua of 4.92 +/- 0.16 x 10^-6 m/s^2 at 1 AU, pointed radially away from the Sun and falling off roughly as 1/r^2. Yet deep imaging (VLT, HST, Gemini) and Spitzer showed no coma, no dust tail, and no detected gas — the usual engine of such accelerations in comets. Explaining it with outgassing would need ~10 kg/s of gas with a gas-to-dust ratio >100x any known comet.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

This is the anomaly that launched the artifact hypothesis: an excess sunward-decaying push with no visible propellant is precisely the signature of solar radiation pressure on a thin, low-mass-per-area object. Loeb called the combination of acceleration plus no coma the single most anomalous fact about 'Oumuamua, and in later essays assigned the natural-comet explanation a low likelihood given the additional fine-tuning (no spin-up torque, no infrared detection) it requires.

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Micheli et al. themselves concluded comet-like outgassing is physically viable if 'Oumuamua has unusual thermal/compositional properties (large grains, low dust). The ISSI team (Nature Astronomy 2019) endorsed volatile outgassing below detection thresholds. Dedicated exotic-ice models followed: hydrogen iceberg (Seligman & Laughlin, ApJL 896, L8, 2020), nitrogen-ice fragment (Desch & Jackson, JGR Planets, 2021), and radiolytic H2 trapped in water ice (Bergner & Seligman, Nature 615, 610, March 2023). A minority view (Katz 2019, arXiv:1904.02218) disputed the acceleration itself, but the 30-sigma detection is broadly accepted.

(2) Acceleration (4.92 +/- 0.16) x 10^-6 m/s^2 confirmed verbatim; ratio = 30.8 so ~30-sigma is accurate; radially anti-solar ~1/r^2 confirmed; ~10 kg/s is Micheli et al.'s own adopted best estimate (their stated range 0.7-140 kg/s); the 'gas-to-dust ratio >100x any known comet' figure is Katz's (arXiv:1904.02218 / Ap&SS 364, 51, 2019) explicit calculation ('at least 100 times greater than that of known Solar System comets') — Katz is already cited in sources, so the claim is grounded, though a pedant could attribute it to Katz explicitly. (3) loeb_quote empty, nothing to verify; loeb_take is fair paraphrase of documented Loeb positions (Bialy & Loeb 2018 ApJL radiation-pressure sail 0.3-0.9 mm; repeated citation of 30-sigma acceleration + no coma as central anomaly; spin-up and Spitzer fine-tuning arguments). (4) Official explanation fairly stated with real papers: Micheli et al. attribute to comet-like outgassing; ISSI team = Nature Astronomy 3, 594-602 (2019), DOI 10.1038/s41550-019-0816-x, matches source URL; Seligman & Laughlin ApJL 896, L8 (2020) H2 ice confirmed; Desch & Jackson JGR Planets 126, e2020JE006807 (March 2021) N2 ice confirmed; Bergner & Seligman Nature 615, 610 (22 March 2023), DOI s41586-022-05687-w confirmed and matches source URL 4. ### O-07 — Bialy-Loeb lightsail hypothesis (the 0.3-0.9 mm sail)

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2018-11-12
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`
- **Loeb Scale (third-party rank):** 4

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Bialy & Loeb (ApJL 868, L1; arXiv 2018-10-26, published 2018-11-12) showed that solar radiation pressure alone reproduces the observed 1/r^2 excess acceleration (Micheli et al. 2018) if 'Oumuamua's mass-to-area ratio is ~0.1 g/cm^2 - i.e., a thin sheet only ~0.3-0.9 mm thick for normal material densities. They calculated such a thin film would survive interstellar dust/gas ablation and tidal/centrifugal (tensile) stresses over galactic distances.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

The paper offered two readings: a naturally produced ultra-thin object of a new class, or an artificial origin - 'a lightsail, floating in interstellar space as debris from advanced technological equipment' - with a 'more exotic' alternative that it was a deliberate probe. Loeb expanded this into 'Extraterrestrial' (Jan 2021), arguing the artifact hypothesis was the most economical explanation of all the anomalies jointly and publicly calling artificial origin a 'serious possibility'; in his August 2025 Loeb Scale paper (arXiv:2508.09167) he retroactively ranked 'Oumuamua Level 4 ('critical threshold' - technological explanation must be considered), versus Level 0 for 2I/Borisov and Level 4 for 3I/ATLAS.

> Alternatively, a more exotic scenario is that 'Oumuamua may be a fully operational probe sent intentionally to Earth vicinity by an alien civilization.
>
> — Bialy & Loeb 2018, ApJL 868, L1 ('Could Solar Radiation Pressure Explain 'Oumuamua's Peculiar Acceleration?'), Section 5 - exact sentence verified against the published text

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Curran (A&A 649, L17, 2021, ''Oumuamua as a light sail: Evidence against artificial origin') showed light sails asymptotically approach a terminal velocity, so a sail with 'Oumuamua's parameters (thickness <~1 mm, mass ~4x10^4 kg) would need roughly half a billion years just to reach the Sun from its closest likely system of origin - making an artificial sail very unlikely. Zhou et al. (A&A 667, A108, 2022) added that a tumbling sail is inconsistent with the data: <1.5% probability of matching the observed lightcurve amplitude and ~0.4% probability of remaining visible in all 55 post-discovery observations. The ISSI team (Nature Astronomy, 2019) found all observations consistent with a purely natural origin, and porous fractal-aggregate models (Moro-Martin 2019; Luu, Flekkoy & Toussaint 2020) offered a natural route to lightsail-like area-to-mass ratios.

Three fixes: (1) loeb_quote was a silent truncation - restored the full sentence beginning "Alternatively, a more exotic scenario is that..."; the paper's actual lightsail wording ("a lightsail, floating in interstellar space as debris from advanced technological equipment") substituted for the invented phrase "a lightsail of artificial origin". (2) official_explanation conflated papers: Curran 2021 (A&A 649, L17) argues terminal-velocity/travel-time (~0.5 Gyr from nearest plausible origin) and extreme thinness/mass constraints, NOT tumbling; the tumbling/brightness-modulation argument is Zhou et al. 2022 (A&A 667, A108: <1.5% lightcurve-amplitude match, 0.4% visibility probability) - both now correctly attributed, Zhou added to sources. ### O-08 — Spitzer infrared non-detection: small and shiny

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2019-06-09
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

### B-01 — Extreme carbon-monoxide enrichment — the most CO-rich comet measured inside 2.5 AU

- **Object:** 2I/Borisov (C/2019 Q4)
- **Date:** 2020-04-20
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`
- **Loeb Scale (third-party rank):** 0

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Two independent teams reported on 2I/Borisov's coma in the weeks after its 2019-12-08 perihelion (q = 2.0065 AU, e = 3.3565). Bodewits et al. (HST/COS ultraviolet, four post-perihelion epochs from 2019-12-11 to 2020-01-13) derived Q(CO) = 6.4–10.7 × 10^26 molecules s^-1 (equivalent to 30–50 kg s^-1) and CO/H2O of at least 173% — 'more than three times higher than previously measured for any comet in the inner (<2.5 au) solar system,' where CO/H2O mostly ranges 0.2–23% with typical values near 4%. Simultaneous Swift/UVOT + HST/COS epochs (2019-12-19/22 and 2020-01-13) gave CO/H2O = 130–155% directly. Cordiner et al. (ALMA, 2019-12-15/16) measured Q(CO) = (4.4 ± 0.7) × 10^26 s^-1 and Q(HCN) = (7.0 ± 1.1) × 10^23 s^-1, giving CO/H2O ≈ 35–105%, HCN/H2O ≈ 0.06–0.16% (ordinary), and CO/HCN = 630 (+200/−340) — higher than any comet previously observed except the peculiar Oort Cloud comet C/2016 R2 (PanSTARRS), whose CO/HCN was 26,400 at rH = 2.8 AU. Excluding C/2016 R2, the next-highest CO/HCN was 238 ± 21 for C/2006 W3 (Christensen) at rH = 3.3 AU. The ALMA/NASA release put Borisov's CO abundance at 9–26× the solar-system comet average.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb has never treated Borisov's CO excess as a technosignature — it is chemistry, not engineering. In his retrospective framing Borisov is the one interstellar object that looked ordinary: a comet whose volatiles simply record a colder, more carbon-rich birth disk than the Sun's. In the 2025 Loeb Scale paper he co-authored (formally the Interstellar Object Significance Scale, IOSS; Eldadi, Tenenbaum & Loeb, arXiv:2508.09167, submitted 2025-08-06) Borisov is assigned Level 0 — fully consistent with natural processes — the lowest score of the three interstellar objects, against Level 4 for both 1I/'Oumuamua and 3I/ATLAS.

Bodewits et al. add that the volatile carbon-to-oxygen ratio is 'nearly six times higher than that of previously measured solar system comets,' and derive three constraints on the host system: it must be chemically distinct from ours, Borisov must have formed beyond a CO snowline (with CO ice condensing alongside H2O rather than trapped in clathrate), and the system must have undergone dynamical stripping of planetesimals from beyond that snowline — an M-dwarf host, whose CO snowline sits near 6.6 AU, fits well. Notably, the papers do NOT treat known CO-rich solar-system comets as equivalent cases: Bodewits et al. explicitly exclude C/2016 R2 (PanSTARRS) from the comparison because its 2.6 AU perihelion meant it 'never reached the inner solar system where water sublimation is fully effective,' no H2O was detected in its coma, and its dominant volatile is most likely CO2 rather than CO. ### B-03 — March 2020 outburst and the transient 'double nucleus' — a textbook comet splitting event

- **Object:** 2I/Borisov (C/2019 Q4)
- **Date:** 2020-03-30
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`
- **Loeb Scale (third-party rank):** 0

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**
