## The briefing

**Short answer:** It behaves like a comet in most ways and like nothing on record in several. None of the anomalies requires design; the argument is about how many coincidences one object is allowed.

### What is not in dispute

3I/ATLAS is the third confirmed interstellar object, after 1I/ʻOumuamua in 2017 and 2I/Borisov in 2019. Its orbit is unambiguously unbound — it came from outside the solar system and it is leaving. It reached perihelion on 2025-10-29 at 1.3566 AU, passed Mars at 0.1939 AU, and passed Jupiter at 0.3588 AU in March 2026. Those are measured positions, not interpretations, and the console draws its trajectory from JPL Horizons vectors rather than from an artist's idea of one.

### What is genuinely strange

A cluster of measurements sit far outside the range of ordinary comets. A sunward anti-tail that persisted before and after perihelion. Nickel emission without the iron that normally accompanies it. Extreme negative polarization. Methanol enriched against hydrogen cyanide at levels beaten by exactly one solar-system comet. Deuterium far above any local object. A trajectory within about 5 degrees of the ecliptic, retrograde, arriving with close passes of three planets. Each has a published measurement behind it, and the register gives each its own case file.

### Why that is not the same as evidence of design

Almost every anomaly has a cometary explanation that works, and several have more than one. The harder problem is that the anomalies are not independent: a single wrong assumption about the nucleus size, or one unusual formation environment, propagates into many of the individual oddities at once. A tally of 24 or 26 separate anomalies overstates the evidence if one cause produces a dozen of them — and that cuts both ways, because a single unifying mechanism is also exactly what a correct explanation looks like.

### Where the argument actually stands

Avi Loeb ranked the object 4 on his own 0-10 scale in July 2025, held it at 4 that December, and reduced it to 3 in March 2026 after the Jupiter pass produced nothing unusual. That is the person making the strongest published case revising downward on evidence. The mainstream position was never that the object is boring — it is that an extreme natural comet from an old, low-metallicity system explains what has been measured without requiring anything new.

### A-01 — Anomalously large size / interstellar mass budget

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-07-08
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Early photometry made 3I/ATLAS far brighter than expected for an interstellar object at ~4.5 AU: if the light came from a bare nucleus (albedo ~0.05), its radius would be ~10 km (~20 km diameter) — a mass Loeb put at roughly a million times 'Oumuamua's and a thousand times Borisov's (his July paper says over ten-million-fold and hundred-thousand-fold vs Borisov's core). Later HST/JWST work capped the nucleus far smaller (Jewitt et al. HST: radius ~0.22-2.8 km; Loeb's final essay uses a 2.6 km diameter). A separate September 2025 Loeb-Cloete-Veres analysis claimed the absence of pre-perihelion non-gravitational acceleration independently forced a mass above ~33 billion tons (diameter >5 km).

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb (arXiv:2507.05881, submitted 2025-07-08) argued the interstellar rocky-material reservoir cannot deliver ~20-km objects at this detection rate — one would expect millions of 'Oumuamua-sized objects for each object of 3I/ATLAS's mass scale, so it is either a comet with a small core (<0.6 km) or a member of an implausibly rare population exceeding the interstellar mass budget. In his November 2025 likelihood ranking he assigned the mass anomaly P<0.001, and he kept the 'mass budget discrepancy' as anomaly #1 of his final 22-item list, arguing planetary-disk reservoirs miss the required mass by orders of magnitude even for the revised 2.6-km diameter.

> [paraphrase] The inferred nucleus diameter of 2.6 kilometers and the number density of its parent population (assuming a natural comet) exceed the mass reservoir of planetary disks around low-metallicity stars by orders of magnitude.
>
> — https://avi-loeb.medium.com/3i-atlas-is-fading-away-leaving-us-to-ponder-over-its-22-mysterious-anomalies-8d1318565f13

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

The brightness is dominated by a dust coma, not the nucleus; Hubble imaging (Jewitt et al., Aug 2025, arXiv:2508.02934) constrained the nucleus radius to <2.8 km (albedo 0.04, lower bound ~0.22 km), eliminating the population problem. Kilometer-scale interstellar comets are consistent with expected ISO size distributions, and Rubin Observatory is expected to find many more. Jason Wright (AstroWright) notes Loeb's later 33-billion-ton mass floor rested on assuming non-gravitational-acceleration measurement precision of 1e-10 AU/day^2 when the actual precision is ~1e-7 — off by ~1000x. Update (Sep 2026): A survey forecast (Albornoz-Montenegro & Fuentes, arXiv:2609.25488, submitted to A&A) still expects Rubin's LSST to find about 0.2 to 100 interstellar objects per year, mostly asteroid-like. For comets like 3I, which it anchors on the single ATLAS detection, it predicts only about 0 to 8 in ten years, and it reads its results as planet formation ejecting more asteroid-like bodies than comets. So 'Rubin will find many more' holds for interstellar objects in general, but 3I-like comets are expected to stay rare. That makes the population question testable: several more 3I-like comets would weaken the rarity point Loeb makes, while none would sit inside the forecast without settling it. Separately, a non-gravitational estimate puts the nucleus radius at 0.45 to 0.65 km (Ahuja et al., arXiv:2609.22596).

### A-05 — Sunward anti-tail / collimated sunward jet

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-08-05
- **Verification:** `CONFIRMED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

The Hubble image of July 21, 2025 showed the dust distribution extended toward the Sun, about ten times longer than wide, with no classical dust tail; a sunward anti-tail re-appeared in July-August and again in November 2025, eventually reaching ~1 million km. Mars HiRISE imaging on Oct 2, 2025 also showed a glowing extension ahead of the object along its direction of motion.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb argued this is a genuine sunward-pointing jet, not the perspective illusion seen in thousands of normal comets (P<0.001 in his Nov 23, 2025 likelihood-ordered list), and that sustaining a collimated sunward plume is more naturally explained by a thruster pointed at the Sun for braking than by sublimating ice.

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Anti-tails and sunward fans are well-documented comet phenomena; Jewitt's HST modeling explains the geometry with large, slow-moving dust grains ejected from the sunlit hemisphere that lag the nucleus, combined with projection effects. AstroWright notes the underlying mechanism has been understood for ~50 years and similar features appear in comets like 17P/Holmes and C/2016 R2.

### A-07 — Nickel emission without iron, and nickel far above the coma's ordinary gas

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-08-28
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

VLT/UVES spectroscopy (early Jul-Aug 21, 2025; ~4.4 to 2.85 AU inbound) detected strong atomic nickel (Ni I) emission with iron (Fe I) undetected — by Aug 21 the Ni production rate was 4.6 +/- 0.7 g/s versus CN at 17.6 +/- 2.0 g/s, rising steeply (Q(Ni) proportional to r^-7.7). Keck II/KCWI imaging (Aug 24, published Oct 2025) confirmed the Ni is concentrated within ~600 km of the nucleus (e-folding radius 593.7 +/- 14.8 km vs 841 km for CN). The Ni/CN ratio (~0.1) is orders of magnitude above the solar-system comet median and higher than 2I/Borisov. Update (Sep 2026): Post-perihelion integral-field spectra from the Hobby-Eberly Telescope on 2025 December 9 (Cochran et al., arXiv:2609.01785, accepted PSJ) show many very strong Fe I and Ni I lines across tens of thousands of km of coma, so by December the iron was plainly present. Nickel still led: the averaged log Q(Ni)/Q(Fe) is 0.19, about 1.5 to 1, similar to values reported at comparable heliocentric distances. A separate team summarising the literature (Ferellec et al., arXiv:2609.05393) calls the Ni/Fe ratio of 3I larger than what is usually measured for Solar System comets. It cites post-perihelion values that match pre-perihelion ones at similar distance. The HET authors call the strong metal lines an important difference from Solar System comets, and exceptional even against 1I and 2I. The fitted parent scale lengths are only about 3,000 km and could be shorter. The authors note that surface temperatures there are too low to vaporise metallic iron or nickel. They conclude the atoms either come straight off the nucleus in metallic form or come from a very short-lived parent, the second being the carbonyl-type route already in this case.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb stressed that release of nickel without iron matches the industrial nickel-refining process via nickel tetracarbonyl, Ni(CO)4 — writing that nickel without iron 'is a signature of industrial production of nickel alloys' and that the carbonyl channel is 'an extremely rare and exotic possibility in comets, whereas it is a standard technology for industrial nickel refining.' He treats the nickel-to-cyanide ratio as a second and separate count against a natural reading: at roughly 0.1 it sits orders of magnitude above the median for solar-system comets and above 2I/Borisov, the only other interstellar comet with comparable measurements. The argument is that the absence of iron says something about the MECHANISM, while the Ni/CN ratio says something about the AMOUNT — that whatever is releasing this nickel is releasing far more of it than ordinary cometary chemistry produces, relative to the gas the coma is otherwise made of.

> is a signature of industrial production of nickel alloys
>
> — Avi Loeb, 'A Steeply-Rising Production of Cyanide and Nickel Without Iron in the Gas Plume Around 3I/ATLAS', Medium, Aug 28, 2025 (verified)

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Ni and Fe emission had already been detected together (at roughly equal abundance) in ~20 solar-system comets and in 2I/Borisov (Manfroid et al. 2021), with low-temperature carbonyl/metal-organic sublimation a leading natural mechanism. For 3I/ATLAS, the observing teams themselves proposed natural in-situ Ni(CO)4 (or Ni-organic/PAH complex) formation and photodissociation near the nucleus — which predicts exactly the observed central concentration — with Fe carbonyl being less volatile and Fe I lines harder to excite/detect at large distances. Fe was subsequently detected as 3I/ATLAS approached the Sun (arXiv 2509.26053), and the Ni/Fe ratio dropped rapidly with decreasing heliocentric distance, trending toward normal comet values. Astronomers (e.g. Jason Wright) call it an extreme but chemically explicable value, not evidence of artificial nickel plating. THE RATIO ARGUMENT HAS A DIFFERENT ANSWER FROM THE IRON ONE, and it turns on the denominator. Ni/CN can be large because there is a great deal of nickel or because there is very little CN, and 3I/ATLAS is independently known to be poor in exactly the gas species that sit in the denominator: its coma is CO2-dominated, with water at only about 4% of the plume, measured over the same inbound stretch. A ratio taken against a depleted denominator is not the same claim as an excess in the numerator, and nothing in these spectra separates the two. Two further features of the measurement cut the same way. The Ni and CN emission do not come from the same volume — nickel is concentrated within an e-folding radius of 593.7 +/- 14.8 km of the nucleus while CN extends to 841 km — so the ratio compares two differently distributed species and partly reports where each is produced rather than how much of each exists. And the whole measurement is heliocentric-distance dependent: these spectra were taken from 4.4 down to 2.85 AU, and the Ni/Fe ratio that looked equally extreme over that same stretch fell rapidly toward ordinary comet values as the object approached the Sun. The extreme numbers live in the far-out regime for both ratios, which is what a volatility-and-excitation effect looks like and is not what a bulk composition looks like. The honest position is that the Ni/CN value is genuinely at the edge of the measured distribution and that the catalog of comets measured this way at these distances is small. Update (Sep 2026): Low-resolution integral-field spectra from the Hobby-Eberly Telescope on 2025 December 9, at 2.03 au, show many very strong Fe I and Ni I lines together. The averaged log Q(Ni)/Q(Fe) is 0.19, so nickel is about 1.5 times iron, close to the high-resolution values at similar distances. By this stage iron is present at comparable abundance. The measured parent scale lengths are very short. The authors conclude the metals leave the nucleus directly in metallic form or come from a very short-lived parent, even though the surface is far too cold to vaporize metallic iron or nickel. The high-resolution teams reached the same conclusion, and the release mechanism is still open. Credit where due: the same authors call 3I exceptional in Fe and Ni even compared with 1I and 2I. Before this century Fe I and Ni I had been seen only in the sungrazers of 1882 and 1965. Better instruments have since found them in many ordinary comets (Manfroid et al.), but there the lines are relatively weak and show only at high spectral resolution, while in 3I they are strong enough to stand out in a low-resolution spectrum. The low-metallicity side of that paradox is softer than it sounds. The modelling that ties 3I's water D/H to a metal-poor birthplace (Furuya et al., arXiv:2609.12370, accepted ApJL) favours metallicities of about half solar or less, which is metal-poor, not metal-free, and such material still carries iron and nickel. Gas-phase Fe and Ni in a coma also trace release chemistry rather than bulk composition, and Fe and Ni lines far from the Sun are the population result already cited in this case. What remains genuinely open is strength. Cochran et al. (arXiv:2609.01785) note that in Solar System comets these lines are weak and seen only at high spectral resolving power, yet in 3I they are obvious even at low resolving power. The VLT team found 3I's combined Ni and Fe production at least an order of magnitude above other comets (arXiv:2509.26053). Their carbonyl scenario explains how and where the atoms are released, not why there are so many of them.

### A-26 — Extreme methanol enrichment (CH3OH/HCN) measured by ALMA

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-09-15
- **Verification:** `CONFIRMED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Roth, Cordiner and colleagues used the Atacama Compact Array of ALMA to detect methanol toward 3I/ATLAS on 2025 August 28, September 18 and 22, and October 1, and hydrogen cyanide on September 12 and 15 — pre-perihelion, at heliocentric distances from 2.6 down to 1.7 AU. Three results came out of it. The two molecules outgassed DIFFERENTLY: HCN production was depleted in the sunward hemisphere of the coma while methanol was enhanced in that same direction. Methanol showed production from coma sources beyond 258 km from the nucleus at 99% confidence, consistent with release from icy grains behaving as miniature comets, though low signal-to-noise on the long baselines that probe the smallest scales stopped the authors from ruling out methanol as a purely nucleus-derived species. HCN, by contrast, was indistinguishable from direct sublimation off the nucleus. And the methanol production rate rose sharply from August through October, with an uptick near the inner edge of the water sublimation zone at 2 AU. The headline number is the ratio: CH3OH/HCN of 124 (+30/-34) on September 12 and 79 (+11/-14) on September 15 — among the most methanol-enriched values measured in any comet at radio wavelengths.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

The enrichment is read as a fuel signature rather than a chemistry result. In the strongest version of the argument, methanol is the propellant of a magnetohydrodynamic drive: it ionises readily into plasma, it can be seeded with the nickel that optical spectroscopy detected, and an MHD thruster would explain in one stroke the collimated sunward jets, the three mini-jets spaced at 120 degrees, and the sideways component of the non-gravitational acceleration. The sunward enhancement is taken as pointing the same way — exhaust directed at the Sun rather than volatiles boiling off it. Combined with the elevated deuterium as reactor fuel, the picture offered is a self-contained propulsion package: deuterium to burn, methanol to throw, nickel alloys in the exhaust.

> Methanol is the perfect propellant here, and ALMA proved that it's bursting with it.
>
> — ANGRY ASTRONAUT · YOUTUBE 2026-03-16 · TRANSCRIBED FROM AUTO-CAPTIONS, NOT A PUBLISHED TEXT

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

The measurement is real, was published in a refereed letter, and the enrichment is genuinely extreme. What it is not is unprecedented — and the paper says so itself in the same sentence. The one object that beats 3I/ATLAS on this measure is C/2016 R2 (PanSTARRS), a comet that formed in OUR solar system. If a native comet can be more methanol-enriched than the interstellar visitor, then this ratio cannot by itself mark an object as foreign, let alone as manufactured. Three further points come straight from the data. First, CH3OH/HCN is a RATIO, and it rises just as readily when HCN is scarce as when methanol is abundant — 3I/ATLAS was already known to be depleted in nitrogen-bearing volatiles, which lifts the quotient without requiring anything unusual in the numerator. Second, the two values are one noisy quantity measured three days apart: 124 (+30/-34) and 79 (+11/-14) overlap inside their own error bars, so quoting them as a span from 70 to 120 reports the scatter as if it were a trend. Third, the extended methanol source is textbook cometary physics rather than an exhaust plume — icy grains lifted into the coma, warmed by sunlight, releasing gas as they go, which is exactly why methanol appears enhanced on the sunward side where the grains are hottest, while HCN coming straight off the nucleus does not. The sharp rise from August to October tracks the inner edge of the water sublimation zone at 2 AU, which is where a sublimation-driven increase is supposed to happen. The honest residue is that this composition is rare and interesting: an object mixed in a different environment from ours, carrying a volatile inventory we have essentially one solar-system analogue for. That is a strong statement about where it formed. It is not evidence of a propellant tank. Update (Sep 2026): The nitrogen side of the ratio is more complicated than 'nitrogen-poor'. VLT/UVES spectra (Aravind et al.) show NH and NH2 strongly depleted from 3.14 au inbound to 4.34 au outbound. Hopkins et al. infer 3I's NH3/H2O at about 0.001 or less, roughly an order of magnitude below Solar System comets. Aravind et al. also cite Biver et al.'s finding that HCN/H2O was depleted at 1.37 au, though they note their own CN/OH ratio is typical. Meanwhile ion spectra of the tail (Ferellec et al.) give N2/CO above 0.023, making 3I N2-RICH compared with Solar System comets. The nitrogen is there; it sits in N2 rather than in ammonia and possibly HCN. A shortage of HCN relative to methanol would still raise CH3OH/HCN without any methanol excess. But the denominator argument now rests on a hydride deficit, not on a general lack of nitrogen, and the typical CN/OH weakens it.
