## The briefing

**Short answer:** 2I/Borisov settled the question of whether interstellar comets look normal: it looked entirely normal. That is what makes the other two interesting, and it is why the register keeps it on file.

### The control case

2I/Borisov arrived in 2019 and behaved like a comet in every respect that was measured — a coma, a tail pointing away from the Sun, a composition inside the range of solar-system comets apart from unusually high carbon monoxide. Nobody argued about it, and its file here is thin by design. It matters enormously anyway: it demonstrates that an object from another star can be completely ordinary, which removes 'it came from far away' as an explanation for strangeness.

### The original argument

1I/ʻOumuamua in 2017 had no coma at all, an extreme elongation, and a non-gravitational acceleration with no visible outgassing to cause it. Bialy and Loeb proposed a thin lightsail. The competing explanations — a hydrogen iceberg, a nitrogen ice fragment, a fractal dust aggregate — each account for the acceleration without any of them being comfortable. It left too fast for anyone to check, which is the whole reason the case never closed.

### Why 3I is different from both

3I/ATLAS is unambiguously active, which rules out the ʻOumuamua reading directly: it has a coma, jets, and measurable outgassing. But its composition and jet geometry are unlike Borisov's, and unlike any solar-system comet in several specific measurements. So it sits between the two earlier objects rather than resembling either, and it was observed for over a year by Webb, Hubble, ALMA and spacecraft near Mars and Jupiter — which is why its file runs to 26 cases and the others do not.

### What the three together suggest

Three objects is not a population, but it is enough to say the visitors are not all alike. If interstellar objects were uniformly ordinary, Borisov would be the template and the other two would need explaining away. If they were uniformly strange, Borisov would be the anomaly. Neither holds. The most defensible reading is that these bodies come from varied environments and carry varied chemistry, and that our sample is far too small to know which of the three is typical.

### O-07 — Bialy-Loeb lightsail hypothesis (the 0.3-0.9 mm sail)

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2018-11-12
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`
- **Loeb Scale (third-party rank):** 4

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Bialy & Loeb (ApJL 868, L1; arXiv 2018-10-26, published 2018-11-12) showed that solar radiation pressure alone reproduces the observed 1/r^2 excess acceleration (Micheli et al. 2018) if 'Oumuamua's mass-to-area ratio is ~0.1 g/cm^2 - i.e., a thin sheet only ~0.3-0.9 mm thick for normal material densities. They calculated such a thin film would survive interstellar dust/gas ablation and tidal/centrifugal (tensile) stresses over galactic distances.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

The paper offered two readings: a naturally produced ultra-thin object of a new class, or an artificial origin - 'a lightsail, floating in interstellar space as debris from advanced technological equipment' - with a 'more exotic' alternative that it was a deliberate probe. Loeb expanded this into 'Extraterrestrial' (Jan 2021), arguing the artifact hypothesis was the most economical explanation of all the anomalies jointly and publicly calling artificial origin a 'serious possibility'; in his August 2025 Loeb Scale paper (arXiv:2508.09167) he retroactively ranked 'Oumuamua Level 4 ('critical threshold' - technological explanation must be considered), versus Level 0 for 2I/Borisov and Level 4 for 3I/ATLAS.

> Alternatively, a more exotic scenario is that 'Oumuamua may be a fully operational probe sent intentionally to Earth vicinity by an alien civilization.
>
> — Bialy & Loeb 2018, ApJL 868, L1 ('Could Solar Radiation Pressure Explain 'Oumuamua's Peculiar Acceleration?'), Section 5 - exact sentence verified against the published text

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Curran (A&A 649, L17, 2021, ''Oumuamua as a light sail: Evidence against artificial origin') showed light sails asymptotically approach a terminal velocity, so a sail with 'Oumuamua's parameters (thickness <~1 mm, mass ~4x10^4 kg) would need roughly half a billion years just to reach the Sun from its closest likely system of origin - making an artificial sail very unlikely. Zhou et al. (A&A 667, A108, 2022) added that a tumbling sail is inconsistent with the data: <1.5% probability of matching the observed lightcurve amplitude and ~0.4% probability of remaining visible in all 55 post-discovery observations. The ISSI team (Nature Astronomy, 2019) found all observations consistent with a purely natural origin, and porous fractal-aggregate models (Moro-Martin 2019; Luu, Flekkoy & Toussaint 2020) offered a natural route to lightsail-like area-to-mass ratios.

### O-06 — Non-gravitational acceleration with no visible coma or tail

- **Object:** 1I/'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **Date:** 2018-06-27
- **Verification:** `CONFIRMED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Micheli et al. (Nature 559, 223; published 2018-06-27) detected, at ~30-sigma significance, a non-gravitational acceleration of 'Oumuamua of 4.92 +/- 0.16 x 10^-6 m/s^2 at 1 AU, pointed radially away from the Sun and falling off roughly as 1/r^2. Yet deep imaging (VLT, HST, Gemini) and Spitzer showed no coma, no dust tail, and no detected gas — the usual engine of such accelerations in comets. Explaining it with outgassing would need ~10 kg/s of gas with a gas-to-dust ratio >100x any known comet.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

This is the anomaly that launched the artifact hypothesis: an excess sunward-decaying push with no visible propellant is precisely the signature of solar radiation pressure on a thin, low-mass-per-area object. Loeb called the combination of acceleration plus no coma the single most anomalous fact about 'Oumuamua, and in later essays assigned the natural-comet explanation a low likelihood given the additional fine-tuning (no spin-up torque, no infrared detection) it requires.

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Micheli et al. themselves concluded comet-like outgassing is physically viable if 'Oumuamua has unusual thermal/compositional properties (large grains, low dust). The ISSI team (Nature Astronomy 2019) endorsed volatile outgassing below detection thresholds. Dedicated exotic-ice models followed: hydrogen iceberg (Seligman & Laughlin, ApJL 896, L8, 2020), nitrogen-ice fragment (Desch & Jackson, JGR Planets, 2021), and radiolytic H2 trapped in water ice (Bergner & Seligman, Nature 615, 610, March 2023). A minority view (Katz 2019, arXiv:1904.02218) disputed the acceleration itself, but the 30-sigma detection is broadly accepted.

### B-01 — Extreme carbon-monoxide enrichment — the most CO-rich comet measured inside 2.5 AU

- **Object:** 2I/Borisov (C/2019 Q4)
- **Date:** 2020-04-20
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`
- **Loeb Scale (third-party rank):** 0

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Two independent teams reported on 2I/Borisov's coma in the weeks after its 2019-12-08 perihelion (q = 2.0065 AU, e = 3.3565). Bodewits et al. (HST/COS ultraviolet, four post-perihelion epochs from 2019-12-11 to 2020-01-13) derived Q(CO) = 6.4–10.7 × 10^26 molecules s^-1 (equivalent to 30–50 kg s^-1) and CO/H2O of at least 173% — 'more than three times higher than previously measured for any comet in the inner (<2.5 au) solar system,' where CO/H2O mostly ranges 0.2–23% with typical values near 4%. Simultaneous Swift/UVOT + HST/COS epochs (2019-12-19/22 and 2020-01-13) gave CO/H2O = 130–155% directly. Cordiner et al. (ALMA, 2019-12-15/16) measured Q(CO) = (4.4 ± 0.7) × 10^26 s^-1 and Q(HCN) = (7.0 ± 1.1) × 10^23 s^-1, giving CO/H2O ≈ 35–105%, HCN/H2O ≈ 0.06–0.16% (ordinary), and CO/HCN = 630 (+200/−340) — higher than any comet previously observed except the peculiar Oort Cloud comet C/2016 R2 (PanSTARRS), whose CO/HCN was 26,400 at rH = 2.8 AU. Excluding C/2016 R2, the next-highest CO/HCN was 238 ± 21 for C/2006 W3 (Christensen) at rH = 3.3 AU. The ALMA/NASA release put Borisov's CO abundance at 9–26× the solar-system comet average.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb has never treated Borisov's CO excess as a technosignature — it is chemistry, not engineering. In his retrospective framing Borisov is the one interstellar object that looked ordinary: a comet whose volatiles simply record a colder, more carbon-rich birth disk than the Sun's. In the 2025 Loeb Scale paper he co-authored (formally the Interstellar Object Significance Scale, IOSS; Eldadi, Tenenbaum & Loeb, arXiv:2508.09167, submitted 2025-08-06) Borisov is assigned Level 0 — fully consistent with natural processes — the lowest score of the three interstellar objects, against Level 4 for both 1I/'Oumuamua and 3I/ATLAS.

> Among the four interstellar objects, only Borisov appeared familiar. It was a comet, not much different from the analogous snowballs found in the Solar system.
>
> — Avi Loeb, 'The Unfamiliar Nature of Interstellar Objects,' Medium, 2022-09-23 (verified verbatim)

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Both teams read the CO excess as a formation-environment signature, not an anomaly. Cordiner et al.: Borisov 'must have formed in a relatively CO-rich environment — probably beyond the CO ice-line in the very cold, outer regions of a distant protoplanetary accretion disk, as part of a population of small, icy bodies analogous to our Solar System's own proto-Kuiper Belt.' Bodewits et al. add that the volatile carbon-to-oxygen ratio is 'nearly six times higher than that of previously measured solar system comets,' and derive three constraints on the host system: it must be chemically distinct from ours, Borisov must have formed beyond a CO snowline (with CO ice condensing alongside H2O rather than trapped in clathrate), and the system must have undergone dynamical stripping of planetesimals from beyond that snowline — an M-dwarf host, whose CO snowline sits near 6.6 AU, fits well. Bodewits et al. call the result 'the first glimpse into the ice content and chemical composition of the protoplanetary disk of another star that is substantially different from our own.' Notably, the papers do NOT treat known CO-rich solar-system comets as equivalent cases: Bodewits et al. explicitly exclude C/2016 R2 (PanSTARRS) from the comparison because its 2.6 AU perihelion meant it 'never reached the inner solar system where water sublimation is fully effective,' no H2O was detected in its coma, and its dominant volatile is most likely CO2 rather than CO. CO-driven comets outside 2.5 AU do commonly show CO exceeding H2O, so a CO-rich nucleus is not unprecedented in kind — but Borisov's ratio inside 2.5 AU, where water sublimation is fully effective, is the record.

### A-12 — Extreme mass combined with extreme speed

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-11-23
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

From the smallness of the non-gravitational acceleration (<15 m/day^2, from 4,022 MPC astrometric observations May 15-Sep 23, 2025) given Webb-measured outgassing of ~150 kg/s, Loeb and colleagues derived (arXiv 2509.21408, Sep 25, 2025) a minimum nucleus mass of ~33 billion tons and minimum diameter ~5 km — roughly a million times 'Oumuamua and a thousand times Borisov — while 3I/ATLAS also moves faster than both (hyperbolic excess speed ~58 km/s vs 26 km/s for 'Oumuamua and 32 km/s for Borisov). After perihelion, Loeb revised the mass estimate down to ~1 billion tons (~100,000x 'Oumuamua) once a small non-gravitational acceleration was detected, but kept the mass-speed pairing on his anomaly lists.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

In his Nov 23, 2025 likelihood-ranked list Loeb assigned this P<0.001 (a Category I 'major' anomaly): known reservoirs of interstellar material should not regularly deliver objects ~a million times more massive than 'Oumuamua, and the mass-speed combination compounds the statistical strain. It appears as anomaly #20 of his final 22 (March 12, 2026). The large nucleus mass also feeds his separate 'mass-budget discrepancy' argument (anomaly #1 of the 22): the implied population of 3I-like objects would exceed the heavy-element mass available in interstellar space.

> The nucleus of 3I/ATLAS is roughly a million times more massive than 1I/`Oumuamua and a thousand times more massive than 2I/Borisov, while moving faster than both
>
> — https://avi-loeb.medium.com/anomalies-of-3i-atlas-organized-by-likelihood-af20fb3b6d21

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Jason Wright (AstroWright, Nov 9, 2025) shows the mass floor rests on claiming a non-gravitational-acceleration precision of 1e-10 AU/day^2 when the real precision is ~1e-7 — a thousand-fold error — and notes Loeb's own non-gravitational-acceleration anomaly uses data at the coarser precision ('he can't have it both ways'); mainstream size estimates keep the nucleus km-scale (Hubble upper limit ~2.8 km diameter). The higher speed is separately explained by mainstream kinematics: 3I/ATLAS's velocity is consistent with an origin in the Galaxy's older, dynamically hotter thick-disk population, implying a kinematic age of ~7-10 Gyr — consistent with later isotope evidence of great age. Update (Sep 2026): A non-gravitational mass estimate by Ahuja et al. (arXiv:2609.22596, MNRAS 552) uses the same equation as Loeb's floor: mass equals outgassing momentum divided by acceleration. It uses the measured JPL non-gravitational terms rather than an upper limit. With JWST CO2 production rates it gives (4.85 +/- 1.06) x 10^11 kg. With water production from OH it gives (3.66 +/- 0.53) x 10^11 kg, and the radius is 0.45 to 0.65 km. That is roughly 70 to 90 times below the 33-billion-ton floor and within a factor of two to three of Loeb's post-perihelion revision to about 1 billion tons, so his revised figure and these agree in order of magnitude. Every estimate of this kind scales directly with the assumed gas outflow speed. Ahuja adopts the speed Biver et al. measured with IRAM, about half the usual value, and the HET/VIRUS coma profiles (Cochran et al.) independently fit only at that halved speed.
