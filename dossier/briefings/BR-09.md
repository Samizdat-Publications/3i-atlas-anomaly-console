## The briefing

**Short answer:** Yes, in the literal sense: something besides gravity has pushed it about 126,000 km off the path gravity alone would have given it, partly out of its orbital plane, and a new paper shows the push is real even with the perihelion data thrown away. What the data cannot say is what is doing the pushing.

### The claim as made

On 1 November 2026 The Angry Astronaut built a video on a new paper, arXiv:2610.38485 by Krolikowska, Spada and Dones: 3I/ATLAS is still changing course and still accelerating, far from the Sun, and nobody knows why. His argument runs in steps. On the way in its motion was purely gravitational. After perihelion something began pushing it, at distances where water ice can no longer boil off. The paper's own activity laws cannot tell the possibilities apart. Too little mass was lost, and the gas leaves too slowly, for outgassing to supply the thrust. So the push is internal, and if it never stops, 3I ends up a relativistic projectile. The paper is real, and on its central point he reports it correctly.

### What the paper actually did

The authors kept only the observations made far from the Sun: 2,646 positions at 6.1 to 3.49 AU on the way in (May to July 2025) and 1,757 at 2.67 to 6.1 AU on the way out (January to April 2026). They threw out the roughly 4,000 positions nearest perihelion, where any push is strongest. A gravity-only orbit fits the inbound arc cleanly. It fits the outbound arc on its own with a fine rms, but the leftover errors are significantly non-Gaussian, and trimming the tails does not fix that. Fit both arcs at once and gravity alone fails: an rms of 0.328 arcsec where about 0.26 is expected. Add a non-gravitational push and it drops to 0.253-0.255, and the systematic trends vanish. Their conclusion, verbatim: the existence of the acceleration is 'considerably better constrained than its physical origin'.

### What the push did, in kilometres

JPL Horizons integrated 3I from its state on 15 May 2025 twice: once with the non-gravitational push of JPL's full-arc solution #54, once with that push switched off. Same Sun, same planets, same Jupiter. By the end of July 2025 the two paths were only 824 km apart, 0.08 arcsec from Earth, below the 0.25 arcsec measurement noise, which is exactly why the inbound data look purely gravitational. By perihelion the gap was 8,000 km, at the Jupiter pass 46,700 km, at the end of the paper's data 56,300 km. On 1 November 2026 it is about 126,000 km, 12.7 arcsec as seen from Earth, some fifty times the noise; about 18,000 km of that is out of the orbital plane. In all the push changed 3I's velocity by about 4.2 m/s, but only 1.75 m/s of that is extra speed; the rest turned its direction. The chart shows the gap growing, and the GRAVITY-ONLY pod in the tracker draws the twin, with the gap exaggerated 2,000 times so it can be seen at solar-system scale, and labelled so.

### Where the claim holds

The push is real, it survives the removal of the near-Sun data, and it is not small next to the measurements. Part of it is a steady push out of the orbital plane: the normal component has the same sign and roughly the same size, -0.69 to -0.83 x1e-8 au/day^2, in every published solution, which is a change of course in the plainest sense. The paper also says the astrometry cannot identify the cause: water-ice and more volatile activity laws fit equally well, and telling them apart would take positions out near 20 AU. And the authors themselves raise the possibility that the leftover non-Gaussian errors mean the push 'varies in magnitude and direction' over the arc. The second chart shows how different those laws are where the data sit, and how little the fits care.

### Where it stretches

Three steps go further than the data. 'It began after perihelion': the outbound positions describe an orbit already bent by the push near the Sun, and the paper says so. In the counterfactual the velocity change was 0.3 m/s at the end of July 2025, 2.3 m/s at perihelion and 4.1 m/s by the Jupiter pass, which is 97% of today's total. The push did nearly all its work within about 3 AU of the Sun; the gap keeps widening afterwards because a 4 m/s difference in velocity keeps adding up, not because the object is still being pushed hard. 'Water cannot sublimate out there, so nothing natural can': carbon dioxide sublimates efficiently far beyond water's 2.8 AU turnover, and JWST measured 3I's coma as CO2-dominated (case A-06); the authors call a CO2/CO law the physically motivated reading, while saying plainly the astrometry does not require it. The video's thrust arithmetic starts from a peak acceleration of 1.1e-6 au/day^2. That is JPL's first, superseded estimate (case A-10); the full-arc #54 value is about 3e-8 au/day^2 at perihelion, some 37 times smaller. (Its force step also slips by a factor of 1,000: 2.5 billion tonnes at 2.2e-5 m/s^2 is 55 meganewtons, not 55 kilonewtons.) The mass-budget step is closer than it looks. The 'under 1%' figure is real, 0.40 to 1.25% of the mass lost as gas (Frincke and Seligman; the video's '0.04%' is a misreading), and the gas really does leave at half the usual speed, 0.30 km/s (Cochran et al.). Real venting is less efficient, so the budget is tight, a factor of one to a few, but the measured loss is a stated lower limit with the dust not yet counted. Tight is not impossible. 'If it never stops': every published fit models the push as fading with distance and fits the data that way. In the counterfactual the velocity change has sat flat near 4.2 m/s since early 2026, though that fade is the model's assumption, not a measurement. Whether a constant push could also fit is a test nobody has published.

JWST NIRSpec observations of Aug 6, 2025 at 3.32 AU (Cordiner et al., arXiv:2508.18209) found a carbon-dioxide-dominated gas coma with CO2/H2O = 7.6 +/- 0.3 (~8:1) — among the highest ratios ever observed in any comet, ~4.5 sigma above the trend for solar-system comets (survey median ~0.12). By mass the plume was ~87% CO2, ~9% CO, and only ~4% water. Update (Oct 2026): the gas left 3I slowly. Fitting VIRUS spectra from 9 December 2025 at 2.03 AU, Cochran et al. (arXiv:2609.01785) found the Haser model fits only if the outflow speed is half that of solar-system comets at the same distance: 0.3 km/s, against about 0.6 km/s from the standard relation. Radio line widths agree: the paper cites 0.37 km/s at 1.36 AU (Biver et al.) and 0.345 km/s (CO) and 0.276 km/s (HCN) at 2.4 AU (Cordiner et al.). The paper cites Biver et al.'s suggestion that the heavy, CO2-rich gas slows the outflow, which would tie this to the CO2 dominance above, but it does not test that itself.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb listed the near-absence of water — the defining volatile of familiar comets — as a minor anomaly at P<0.1 in his 13-item likelihood-ranked list (Nov 23, 2025), suggesting the 'gas plume' might not be cometary sublimation at all but sunlight releasing ices and dust accumulated on the surface of a technological object during its interstellar journey. It is not a separate item in his final 22-anomaly list (Mar 12, 2026), compiled after post-perihelion water detections.

> The gas plume surrounding 3I/ATLAS contains only 4% water by mass, a dominant constituent of familiar solar-system comets.

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

A CO2-rich nucleus is natural for a body formed near its parent disk's CO2 snowline or processed by galactic cosmic rays over billions of years (arXiv:2510.26308); at >3 AU water sublimates inefficiently while CO2 vents freely. Water/OH production rose strongly closer to the Sun — MeerKAT detected 1665/1667 MHz OH absorption on Oct 24, 2025 (ATel #17473), and SOHO/SWAN measured ~3.2e29 water molecules/s near perihelion — and interstellar comets are expected to show compositional diversity. Update (Sep-Oct 2026): Four papers approach the CO2 excess from different sides. Cochran et al. (Hobby-Eberly/VIRUS) find the coma gas leaving at about 0.30 km/s, half the speed expected near 2 au, in line with IRAM and ALMA radio velocities, and cite Biver et al.'s suggestion that heavy CO2-rich gas is the cause. Barry-Taylor models galactic cosmic rays converting CO into CO2 toward a laboratory equilibrium CO/CO2 of about 0.1. Fitted to 3I's measured ratios, the model gives an initial CO/CO2 of about 1.5 and an age of at least 0.6 Gyr, which supports processing as the source of the extra CO2 and formation outside the CO ice line. Aravind et al. (VLT/UVES) find water estimated from forbidden oxygen lines exceeding the OH-based value except near perihelion, which they read as CO2 feeding the coma chemistry. One limit cuts against the natural reading. Hopkins et al.'s chemical model correctly recovers 3I's metal-poor origin from its ammonia, yet the same model predicts 3I should be CO2-POOR compared with Solar System comets. The authors call this inconsistent with observations. They also say it is unclear whether cosmic-ray processing could raise CO2 that far while keeping CO at ordinary levels. The size of the CO2 excess is still not fully explained.

### A-12 — Extreme mass combined with extreme speed

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-11-23
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

From the smallness of the non-gravitational acceleration (<15 m/day^2, from 4,022 MPC astrometric observations May 15-Sep 23, 2025) given Webb-measured outgassing of ~150 kg/s, Loeb and colleagues derived (arXiv 2509.21408, Sep 25, 2025) a minimum nucleus mass of ~33 billion tons and minimum diameter ~5 km — roughly a million times 'Oumuamua and a thousand times Borisov — while 3I/ATLAS also moves faster than both (hyperbolic excess speed ~58 km/s vs 26 km/s for 'Oumuamua and 32 km/s for Borisov). After perihelion, Loeb revised the mass estimate down to ~1 billion tons (~100,000x 'Oumuamua) once a small non-gravitational acceleration was detected, but kept the mass-speed pairing on his anomaly lists.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

In his Nov 23, 2025 likelihood-ranked list Loeb assigned this P<0.001 (a Category I 'major' anomaly): known reservoirs of interstellar material should not regularly deliver objects ~a million times more massive than 'Oumuamua, and the mass-speed combination compounds the statistical strain. It appears as anomaly #20 of his final 22 (March 12, 2026). The large nucleus mass also feeds his separate 'mass-budget discrepancy' argument (anomaly #1 of the 22): the implied population of 3I-like objects would exceed the heavy-element mass available in interstellar space.

> The nucleus of 3I/ATLAS is roughly a million times more massive than 1I/`Oumuamua and a thousand times more massive than 2I/Borisov, while moving faster than both
>
> — https://avi-loeb.medium.com/anomalies-of-3i-atlas-organized-by-likelihood-af20fb3b6d21

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Jason Wright (AstroWright, Nov 9, 2025) shows the mass floor rests on claiming a non-gravitational-acceleration precision of 1e-10 AU/day^2 when the real precision is ~1e-7 — a thousand-fold error — and notes Loeb's own non-gravitational-acceleration anomaly uses data at the coarser precision ('he can't have it both ways'); mainstream size estimates keep the nucleus km-scale (Hubble upper limit ~2.8 km diameter). The higher speed is separately explained by mainstream kinematics: 3I/ATLAS's velocity is consistent with an origin in the Galaxy's older, dynamically hotter thick-disk population, implying a kinematic age of ~7-10 Gyr — consistent with later isotope evidence of great age. Update (Sep 2026): A non-gravitational mass estimate by Ahuja et al. (arXiv:2609.22596, MNRAS 552) uses the same equation as Loeb's floor: mass equals outgassing momentum divided by acceleration. It uses the measured JPL non-gravitational terms rather than an upper limit. With JWST CO2 production rates it gives (4.85 +/- 1.06) x 10^11 kg. With water production from OH it gives (3.66 +/- 0.53) x 10^11 kg, and the radius is 0.45 to 0.65 km. That is roughly 70 to 90 times below the 33-billion-ton floor and within a factor of two to three of Loeb's post-perihelion revision to about 1 billion tons, so his revised figure and these agree in order of magnitude. Every estimate of this kind scales directly with the assumed gas outflow speed. Ahuja adopts the speed Biver et al. measured with IRAM, about half the usual value, and the HET/VIRUS coma profiles (Cochran et al.) independently fit only at that halved speed.
