## 3I/ATLAS — 47 events

### 2025-05-07 — TESS pre-discovery detections begin

- **kind:** `observation`

NASA's TESS spacecraft serendipitously imaged 3I/ATLAS from 2025-05-07 to 2025-06-02, 55 days before discovery, at roughly 6.4 AU from the Sun. Precovery photometry suggests the object was already active at ~6 AU, implying sublimation of ices more volatile than water.

### 2025-07-01 — Discovery by ATLAS, Rio Hurtado, Chile

- **kind:** `discovery`

The ATLAS survey telescope at Rio Hurtado, Chile (station W68) discovered the object at apparent magnitude ~18, 4.51 AU from the Sun and 3.50 AU from Earth, inbound at ~61 km/s relative to the Sun.

**Sources:** <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3I/ATLAS> · <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/3i-atlas/>

### 2025-07-02 — Confirmed interstellar: designated 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)

- **kind:** `discovery`

Follow-up astrometry confirmed a strongly hyperbolic orbit (eccentricity 6.141, hyperbolic excess velocity ~58 km/s, faster than 1I/'Oumuamua at 26 km/s and 2I/Borisov at 32 km/s). The Minor Planet Center announced it as the third confirmed interstellar object; Nordic Optical Telescope and Teide Observatory confirmed cometary activity the same day.

**Sources:** <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3I/ATLAS> · <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/3i-atlas/>

### 2025-07-16 — Loeb raises 'alien technology' hypothesis; anomaly list begins

- **kind:** `statement`

Avi Loeb (with Adam Hibberd and Adam Crowl) posted the arXiv paper 'Is the Interstellar Object 3I/ATLAS Alien Technology?' (arXiv:2507.12213, v1 2025-07-16), starting a running numbered list of anomalies (retrograde ecliptic-aligned trajectory ~0.2% probability, fine-tuned Mars/Jupiter arrival geometry, etc.) that grew to 22 items by March 2026. NASA/ESA and most astronomers maintained it is a natural comet throughout.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12213> · <https://avi-loeb.medium.com/3i-atlas-is-fading-away-leaving-us-to-ponder-over-its-22-mysterious-anomalies-8d1318565f13> · <https://sites.psu.edu/astrowright/2025/11/09/loebs-3i-atlas-anomalies-explained/>

### 2025-07-21 — Hubble images constrain nucleus size

- **kind:** `observation`

Hubble Space Telescope imaging constrained the nucleus diameter to 0.32-5.6 km and revealed a sunward dust fan / anti-solar tail. Later non-gravitational-acceleration analyses pointed to a sub-kilometer nucleus, roughly 0.8-1.1 km diameter and mass ~1.6e11 kg if the acceleration is driven by CO2 outgassing (Thoss, Loeb & Burkert).

**Sources:** <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3I/ATLAS> · <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/3i-atlas/> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18341> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15735>

### 2025-08-06 — JWST: unusually CO2-rich composition

- **kind:** `observation`

JWST NIRSpec observations (results announced 2025-08-25) found a coma unusually rich in carbon dioxide, CO2/H2O mixing ratio 8.0±1.0, among the highest ever measured, with CO2 at 129±1 kg/s, water 6.6±0.2 kg/s, CO 14.0±0.9 kg/s, plus water ice and carbonyl sulfide.

**Sources:** <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3I/ATLAS>

### 2025-08-21 — SPHEREx maps giant CO2 coma; VLT confirms cyanide and nickel

- **kind:** `observation`

NASA's SPHEREx (observing 2025-08-08 to 08-12) reported a CO2 gas coma spanning at least 348,000 km in radius (~70 kg/s CO2) plus water ice. Concurrent VLT spectroscopy confirmed cyanide (17.6±2.0 g/s) alongside atomic nickel (4.6±0.7 g/s, first detected 2025-07-20) with no accompanying iron vapor, the nickel-without-iron signature became Loeb anomaly material, though nickel-carbonyl chemistry in normal comets can explain it.

**Sources:** <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3I/ATLAS> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15469>

### 2025-10-03 — Mars close approach: 0.194 AU; imaged by Mars orbiters

- **kind:** `close_approach`

3I/ATLAS passed 0.194 AU (29.0 million km) from Mars at 04:38 UT. NASA's Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter HiRISE imaged it at ~30 km/pixel (a diffuse, comet-consistent smear), ESA's ExoMars TGO CaSSIS captured the coma in 5-second exposures, and MAVEN detected hydrogen in the UV. ### 2025-10-21 — Solar conjunction: unobservable from Earth

- **kind:** `status`

### 2025-10-29 — Perihelion: 1.36 AU from the Sun

- **kind:** `close_approach`

Perihelion at 11:45 UT on 2025-10-29 at 1.356 AU (203 million km), between the orbits of Earth and Mars, moving at its maximum speed of ~68 km/s. It brightened faster near perihelion than typical comets, item 22 on Loeb's anomaly list, attributed by most astronomers to hypervolatile-driven outgassing.

**Sources:** <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3I/ATLAS> · <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/3i-atlas/>

### 2025-11-08 — Post-conjunction reemergence in the morning sky

- **kind:** `observation`

Ground-based observers recovered the comet after solar conjunction; astrophotographer Michael Jaeger imaged the revived dust tail on 2025-11-08. ### 2026-06-30 — Loeb co-authors a panspermia study: and rules out the directed version

- **kind:** `statement`

Kakharov & Loeb ask whether an interstellar object like 3I/ATLAS could carry life between stars, either naturally or on purpose. They find natural panspermia plausible: microbes can survive at very low metabolic rates in ice films and veins, though matching the JWST methane production by frozen survival metabolism alone would need something like 10^14-10^15 kg of biomass. Directed panspermia they rule out on energy grounds, a direct 60 km/s impact releases 1.8x10^9 J/kg, hundreds of times the specific energy of TNT, which would destroy any biological capsule. Notable for the register because it is Loeb publishing a negative result about a deliberate-technology scenario.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00202>

### 2026-07-02 — FAST radio search: no periodic technosignature either

- **kind:** `observation`

A team using China's Five-hundred-metre Aperture Spherical Telescope ran an L-band multibeam search aimed at 3I/ATLAS, specifically for periodically modulated signals, a signal type earlier searches had not covered, using canonical polyadic decomposition to separate a genuine centre-beam source from interference. No credible artificial periodic radio technosignature was detected above 0.146 W. This is the second independent radio null result on the object after the Allen Telescope Array study, and it closes a different part of the search space rather than repeating the first.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01666>

### 2026-07-09 — JUICE/MAJIS peer-reviewed: CO2 drove the activity, plus tentative organics

- **kind:** `observation`

The ESA JUICE results announced by press release in April arrive as a paper. MAJIS observed 3I/ATLAS from 0.5 to 5.56 microns between 2 and 25 November 2025, just after perihelion, detecting water fluorescence at 2.7 microns and CO2 at 4.3 microns. Water production fell from 8x10^28 to 4x10^28 molecules per second across the period while the CO2/H2O ratio held near 10%, and the heliocentric trend indicates activity controlled by solar heating with CO2 playing the major role. Broad emission at 3.2-3.6 microns cannot be explained by common cometary CH-bearing volatiles; the authors read it as aliphatic C-H groups and tentative evidence for complex organic material released from dust grains.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08603>

### 2026-07-17 — Crossing Saturn's orbital distance, hidden in conjunction

- **kind:** `status`

In mid-July 2026 3I/ATLAS was about 9.25 AU from the Sun (~1.38 billion km; 10.25 AU from Earth per JPL Horizons), reaching Saturn's ~9.5 AU orbital distance in late July while hidden in solar conjunction (solar elongation ~9 deg), unobservable and fainter than magnitude 20. It is escaping on its hyperbolic trajectory at ~58 km/s excess velocity, nearly symmetric to its inbound path; it will pass the inner Oort cloud region (~2,000 AU) around the year 2189 and leave the Sun's influence entirely in a few thousand years. Consensus verdict: the most-studied interstellar object ever, and a natural, though unusually CO2-rich, nickel-emitting, and ancient (possibly 7-14 billion years old), comet.

**Sources:** <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3I/ATLAS> · <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/3i-atlas/> · <https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/>

### 2026-08-18 — Inbound campaign published: carbon-chain poor, and a persistent sunward jet

- **kind:** `observation`

A multi-band monitoring campaign covering July to September 2025, Faulkes and Maidanak imaging with SALT and Nordic Optical Telescope spectroscopy, reports a stable reddish colour with no secular variation on the way in, carbon-chain depletion with CN the only prominent molecular emission, and a persistent sunward jet at a position angle of about 280 degrees, read as sustained localized outgassing from a high-latitude active region. That last point bears directly on case A-05: an independent campaign confirms the sunward feature is real and long-lived, and attributes it to a fixed active area rather than anything exotic. Light-scattering modelling matches dense aggregates of organic matter and silicates, with water-ice sublimation too weak to change the optical properties.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18371>

### 2026-09-01 — HET/VIRUS maps nickel and iron 85,000 km into the coma

- **kind:** `observation`

Cochran and colleagues observed 3I/ATLAS on 2025 December 9 with the VIRUS integral-field spectrograph on the Hobby-Eberly Telescope, sampling the coma every 1,327 km out to about 85,000 km sunward. Alongside the usual cometary molecules the spectra show many very strong Fe I and Ni I lines, which the authors call an important difference from Solar System comets. The averaged log Q(Ni)/Q(Fe) is 0.19 (nickel about 1.5 times iron), similar to values Hutsemekers et al. report at comparable heliocentric distances. The radial profiles fit only if the gas flows out at about half the speed seen in Solar System comets at the same distance (0.3 km/s adopted). Biver et al. have proposed a heavy, CO2-rich gas as the cause, and the authors repeat that suggestion. Accepted in The Planetary Science Journal.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01785>

### 2026-09-04 — Post-perihelion polarimetry: the extreme negative branch persists and deepens

- **kind:** `observation`

Gray and colleagues extended polarimetry with FORS2 at the VLT, ALFOSC at the Nordic Optical Telescope and FoReRo2 at a 2 m telescope to phase angles of 1 to 30 degrees, the widest coverage obtained for any interstellar object. The unusual polarization survived perihelion unchanged in character, with the R-band minimum now pinned at -2.94% at 5.5 degrees phase and the inversion angle at 16.1 degrees. The curve matches small trans-Neptunian objects (Huya, Ixion, Varuna, 1999 DE9) and the Centaur Pholus, and at 30 degrees 3I is about 3.5 percentage points more polarized than typical high-polarization comets. Modelling favours porous aggregates larger than 10 microns built of weakly absorbing submicron grains, with a dust albedo near 0.7, about ten times that of 67P's dust.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04932>

### 2026-09-04 — Plasma-tail ions show 3I/ATLAS is nitrogen-rich

- **kind:** `observation`

WEAVE integral-field spectroscopy on the William Herschel Telescope on 2025 November 30 and December 2 detected five ions in the anti-solar tail: N2+, CO+, CO2+, H2O+ and CH+. The N2+/CO+ ratio sets a lower limit of N2/CO > 0.023 +/- 0.001, which the authors call N2-rich compared to Solar System comets and similar to the few N2-rich comets measured. Rosetta's in-situ value for 67P was first ~0.006 and later revised to 0.03. NH2 is still undetected, which points to nitrogen stored as N2 rather than ammonia, the same pattern as C/2016 R2. The authors link it to cold formation conditions, near 20 K by the reasoning applied to 67P.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05393>

### 2026-09-11 — Astrochemical models: the extreme water D/H fits a metal-poor birth cloud

- **kind:** `statement`

Furuya, Cordiner and colleagues ran gas-ice astrochemical models from molecular cloud to dense core to test whether the water D/H of about 0.01 in 3I/ATLAS fits the low-metallicity origin suggested by its carbon isotopes. Lower metallicity boosts deuteration of H3+ and its transfer into water ice; the observed value is most readily reproduced at half solar metallicity or below and cloud densities near 10^4 per cubic cm, provided the cosmic-ray ionization rate stays below 10^-15 per second. One solar-metallicity parameter set also exceeds 0.01, so low metallicity is favoured rather than required. Accepted in ApJ Letters.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12370>

### 2026-09-16 — JWST: water ice in the coma before and long after perihelion

- **kind:** `observation`

Protopapa and colleagues report JWST NIRSpec spectra from 2025 August 6, 2025 December 22 and 2026 April 1, at 3.3, 2.4 and 5.7 AU from the Sun. A broad 3 micron water-ice band appears with weak or missing 1.5 and 2.0 micron bands. The best model is submicron-to-micron icy aggregates mixed with refractory material. The band was 77% deep in August and 94% deep in April, when a second population of larger ice-rich grains and a Fresnel-like feature consistent with crystalline ice appeared. The authors allow either crystalline ice at both epochs or a change from amorphous to crystalline. The December spectrum was not modelled because water gas, organics, Ni I emission and warm dust overlap the band, but the authors describe the band shape as largely unchanged between epochs. They place 3I at the refractory-rich end of a continuum running through Solar System comae and mid-sized trans-Neptunian objects.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19286>

### 2026-09-17 — Thermophysical model ties the late methane to cosmic-ray processing

- **kind:** `statement`

Rawat and Acharyya coupled a nucleus thermophysical model to galactic cosmic-ray processing and ran 12,000 models against the measured H2O, CO, CO2 and CH4 production. The viable models start from a volatile-rich body formed below 20 K beyond the CO snowline and need an effective cosmic-ray exposure of 0.87 to 3.1 billion years. Radiation strips methane from near-surface layers and converts some CO into CO2, while methane trapped deeper in the water ice survives and is released once post-perihelion heating reaches it. The authors treat the pre-perihelion methane signal as a weak constraint because it is blended with ethane bands. Accepted in ApJ.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19694>

### 2026-09-18 — Indian telescopes: carbon chemistry turns typical after perihelion, nucleus about 0.55 km

- **kind:** `observation`

Ahuja and colleagues monitored 3I/ATLAS after perihelion, from 2025 November 16, with the 0.5, 1.2 and 2.5 m Physical Research Laboratory telescopes, the 2 m Himalayan Chandra Telescope and a 0.132 m refractor. CN, C2 and C3 production rates and ratios moved from carbon-depleted before perihelion to carbon-typical after it, which they attribute to fresh subsurface material. Broadband colours (B-V 1.03, V-R 0.59) stayed nearly constant and consistent with pre-perihelion values. They are redder than the Sun and long-period comets and match ultrared-material-rich Solar System objects. The JPL non-gravitational acceleration, with an outflow speed halved per Biver et al., gives a mass of 4.85 x 10^11 kg from CO2 and 3.66 x 10^11 kg from water, and a nucleus radius of 0.45 to 0.65 km. Published in MNRAS 552.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22596>

### 2026-09-21 — Survey forecast: LSST may find 0 to 8 more comets like 3I/ATLAS in ten years

- **kind:** `statement`

Albornoz-Montenegro and Fuentes model how the size distribution of interstellar objects sets detection rates in TESS, Pan-STARRS, ATLAS and Rubin's LSST, anchoring the comet population on the single ATLAS detection of 3I/ATLAS. They estimate about 0 to 8 detections of 3I-like comets by LSST in ten years and about one by TESS over its mission, independent of the nucleus size distribution. Fitting 1I/'Oumuamua and 3I/ATLAS from the same size distribution, they suggest planet formation ejects more asteroid-like bodies than comets. Submitted to A&A.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25488>

### 2026-09-22 — VLT/UVES: a severe ammonia deficit sets 3I apart from Solar System comets

- **kind:** `observation`

Aravind et al. (accepted in A&A) followed the coma with VLT/UVES from August 2025 at 3.14 au to February 2026 at 4.34 au. NH and NH2 are strongly depleted. The C2/CN ratio shows carbon-chain depletion and drifts with distance, which the authors read as a coma effect such as hyperactivity, not a change in composition. Water production rises steeply, with a heliocentric index of 8.47 +/- 0.23. In abundance-ratio space 3I sits outside the canonical Solar System comet cluster. It is nearest the strongly carbon-chain-depleted comets, including the Giacobini-Zinner type, but more depleted in NH and C3 relative to CN, and its unusually high CN/OH ratio sets it apart from that group as well. The authors read this as formation in an exceptionally cold natal environment.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26577>

### 2026-09-25 — Ammonia as a metallicity clock: 3I's parent star was metal-poor

- **kind:** `discovery`

Hopkins, Bannister, Bitsch, Lintott and Forbes combined a chemical partition model with APOGEE DR17 stellar abundances and found that an interstellar object's ammonia content tracks its parent star's metallicity. Ammonia was not measured directly, so they inferred 3I's NH3/H2O as about 0.001 or less, roughly an order of magnitude below Solar System comets. From that they place its parent star at [Fe/H] between -0.8 and 0.0, which corroborates the velocity- and isotope-based ages by an independent route. The same model predicts 3I should be CO2-poor, the opposite of what was observed. The authors call this inconsistent with observations, offer cosmic-ray conversion of CO to CO2 as a possible explanation, and say it is unclear whether that could work while CO stays at ordinary levels. They also note giant planets are rare around metal-poor stars, so 3I was probably ejected by another mechanism such as a stellar flyby.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30827>

### 2026-10-04 — Near-opposition polarimetry: the deep negative branch holds, but the dust may be dark

- **kind:** `observation`

Choi et al. measured polarization with the 1.6 m Pirka Telescope down to a phase angle of 0.70 degrees. Their fit to the combined post-perihelion curve gives a minimum of -3.04 +/- 0.028% at 5.98 degrees and an inversion angle of 16.3 degrees. That agrees with Gray et al.'s post-perihelion -2.94% at 5.52 degrees, which Gray et al. attribute mainly to better small-phase coverage than in the first -2.7% fit, not to dust evolution. The branch persisted across perihelion. A visible-to-mid-infrared energy distribution from HST, VLT, SPHEREx and JWST gives dark dust with an effective albedo of about 0.04, a size index of 3.0 to 3.3, and a minor amorphous-silicate component. Dirty water-ice models fail to reproduce the thermal emission. The polarimetry favours a highly porous scattering structure. Gray et al. also favour porous aggregates, but of weakly absorbing grains that make the dust brighter than in typical comets, so what makes the branch this deep is still open.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04919> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04932>

### 2026-10-06 — Spada, Krolikowska & Dones published: the push is real, its sideways part is not stable

- **kind:** `observation`

The orbit study first posted in March (arXiv:2603.00782) appears in Astronomy & Astrophysics. Fitting gravity-only, symmetric, delayed-peak and asymmetric outgassing models to the full astrometry, it finds a non-gravitational acceleration is required, and that systematic errors in the data and in the outgassing model exceed the formal fit errors. The transverse component is the least stable parameter, which is the point case A-24 turns on.

**Sources:** <https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202659645> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00782>

### 2026-10-12 — Mass-loss audit revised: gas alone took 0.4 to 1.25% of the nucleus

- **kind:** `observation`

Frincke and Seligman's third version (accepted by MNRAS) integrates every published 3I production rate into a gas-only mass loss of 1.83 to 5.77 x 10^10 kg, about 1.7 to 5.2 m of surface, or 0.40 to 1.25% of the initial mass for an assumed 1.3 km radius and 0.5 g/cm3 density. Adding dust at dust-to-gas ratios of 1 to 4 raises the loss to roughly 3.7 x 10^10 to 3.0 x 10^11 kg and erosion beyond 10 m. The authors present all figures as lower limits; earlier versions gave 0.10 to 1.13% (May) and 0.12 to 0.53% (July).

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01377>

### 2026-10-15 — ZTF catches a short-lived icy-grain halo and a blue outer coma

- **kind:** `observation`

Liu, Zhang, Liu and Hui (ApJL 1008, L26) used ZTF imaging to estimate dust mass-loss rates of about 123 to 203 kg/s before perihelion and 596 to 1,412 kg/s after. In late November 2025 the outer coma was anomalously blue and steeply fading, and that structure was gone by 2026 January 18. They model it as icy grains of 5 to 10 microns with about 0.1% absorbing carbon, destroyed over a projected length of 6.4 to 7.2 x 10^4 km.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21206>

### 2026-10-17 — Loeb adds a machine-control scale and rates 3I/ATLAS C0

- **kind:** `statement`

In a Medium post and a co-authored paper (Volkan Duran, Omer Eldadi and Loeb, posted on his Harvard site, not arXiv), Loeb adds a four-step SETAI Control Scale (C0 to C3) on top of the Loeb Scale. It asks whether an object's recorded behaviour rules out biological direction, scripted automation or adaptive autonomy. Applied to the three known interstellar objects, it leaves every published Loeb Scale level unchanged. 3I/ATLAS keeps the Level 4 that Eldadi et al. assigned in 2025, and the paper states that it carries that level forward only to demonstrate the extension, without re-evaluating it. 3I/ATLAS and 1I/'Oumuamua get C0 because neither showed contingent behaviour. 2I/Borisov, at Level 0, never reaches the control question. The authors say C0 'does not rule out technological origin' and is not a verdict against SETAI. For 3I they name what would raise C: a change in trajectory or attitude timed to a close planetary approach that outgassing models fitted to the rest of the passage do not predict. They also argue that a rendezvous mission would supply the behavioural record interstellar objects have lacked.

**Sources:** <https://avi-loeb.medium.com/extending-the-loeb-scale-to-the-search-for-extraterrestrial-artificial-intelligence-setai-6f7bfc7cf2e3> · <https://lweb.cfa.harvard.edu/~loeb/DEL.pdf>

### 2026-10-23 — Keck and Gemini publish a ten-month benchmark record of 3I

- **kind:** `observation`

Bolin et al. report 14 imaging and 8 spectroscopic optical epochs plus near-infrared data from 2025 July to 2026 May. Colours stay red and unchanged across perihelion (g-r = 0.732 +/- 0.029), and activity falls faster outbound (Afrho proportional to r^-3.34) than it rose inbound (r^-2.10). Dust speeds imply 2.7 x 10^9 to 1.0 x 10^10 kg of 1 micron to 1 mm grains lost, a 0.26 to 0.99 m global layer. The compact sunward feature seen beside an antisolar tail on 2025 August 27 weakened through September. C2/CN nearly doubled, from 0.257 to 0.481, and no water-ice absorption survives their calibration tests.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32972>

### 2026-10-23 — Deuterated methane published: 14 times 67P

- **kind:** `observation`

Roth, Cordiner and colleagues' JWST measurement of deuterated methane (CH3D) in 3I's coma was published in Nature Astronomy. Methane D/H is 3.33 +/- 0.31%, higher than any methane value measured in the solar system and a factor of 14 +/- 2 above 67P, the only other comet with a CH3D detection. As in 67P, methane is more deuterated than water. The authors read it as ices formed in a cold, low-metallicity environment under strong cosmic-ray irradiation.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20445> · <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-026-03002-5>

### 2026-10-27 — Second JWST/MIRI study: 3I's silicates are almost entirely amorphous

- **kind:** `observation`

Thomas et al. took a JWST/MIRI low-resolution spectrum on 2025 December 16 at 2.21 au. They found a smooth 10-micron silicate feature unlike those of Solar System comets, best fitted by amorphous pyroxene (pyroxene to olivine about 180:1). The best-fitting crystalline fraction is zero with a 5.3% upper limit, in line with Belyakov et al.'s 3.1% from MIRI medium resolution, and a 6.83-micron feature points to aliphatic C-H material. The authors conclude the dust saw little thermal processing, while noting that diffuse interstellar dust is olivine-type, so this is not a simple interstellar-medium match.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37540>

### 2026-10-28 — The push without the perihelion data: robust detection, cause unconstrained

- **kind:** `observation`

Krolikowska, Spada and Dones refit 3I's orbit using only observations far from the Sun, 6.1 to 3.49 AU inbound and 2.67 to 6.1 AU outbound. Gravity alone fits the inbound arc but cannot fit both arcs together (rms 0.328 against about 0.26 arcsec); every non-gravitational model brings it to about 0.254 and removes the residual trends. Water-ice and more volatile activity laws fit equally well, so the astrometry confirms the push but cannot name its cause. The outbound residuals stay non-Gaussian, which the authors say may mean the push varied in magnitude and direction.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38485>

## 1I/'Oumuamua — 16 events

### 2017-09-09 — Perihelion at 0.256 AU (pre-discovery)

- **kind:** `close_approach`

'Oumuamua made its closest approach to the Sun at 0.256 AU (inside Mercury's orbit), moving at ~87.7 km/s at perihelion, far too fast for the Sun to retain it. Nothing on Earth saw it: perihelion occurred ~40 days before discovery, so the entire inbound leg and the solar flyby were unobserved. This gap later mattered enormously, every acceleration measurement and every thermal model had to extrapolate back through a perihelion no one witnessed.

**Sources:** <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/oumuamua/> · <https://sci.esa.int/web/solar-system/-/60465-oumuamua-journey-through-our-solar-system>

### 2017-10-14 — Closest approach to Earth at ~0.162 AU (still undetected)

- **kind:** `close_approach`

The object passed ~0.16175 AU (~24.2 million km, about 63 lunar distances) from Earth, its minimum Earth distance, five days before anyone found it. It was detected only on the outbound leg, already receding, which compressed the entire global observing campaign into a few frantic weeks before it faded beyond reach of ground-based telescopes.

**Sources:** <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/oumuamua/> · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1I/%CA%BBOumuamua>

### 2017-10-19 — Discovery by Robert Weryk / Pan-STARRS1

- **kind:** `discovery`

Robert Weryk found the fast-moving ~20th-magnitude object (it had peaked at magnitude 19.7 the night before) in Pan-STARRS1 survey images from Haleakala, Hawaii, while searching for near-Earth asteroids. Follow-up astrometry (including pre-discovery images from Oct 18) quickly showed an eccentricity of ~1.2, the first solidly hyperbolic small body ever recorded, meaning it came from outside the Solar System. Initially designated comet C/2017 U1 (PANSTARRS).

**Sources:** <https://www.minorplanetcenter.net/mpec/K17/K17V17.html> · <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/oumuamua/> · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1I/%CA%BBOumuamua>

### 2017-11-06 — Designation saga ends: first-ever 'I' class: 1I/'Oumuamua

- **kind:** `status`

A two-week identity crisis: designated comet C/2017 U1 at discovery, reclassified asteroid A/2017 U1 on Oct 25 when deep imaging showed no coma or tail, then on Nov 6 the Minor Planet Center (MPEC 2017-V17) created an entirely new designation class, 'I' for interstellar, making it 1I/'Oumuamua, with the IAU-approved Hawaiian name meaning roughly 'a messenger from afar arriving first' (suggested by the Pan-STARRS team). No object had ever forced the IAU to invent a new category before.

**Sources:** <https://www.minorplanetcenter.net/mpec/K17/K17V17.html> · <https://www.space.com/38798-first-interstellar-object-name-oumuamua.html>

### 2017-11-20 — VLT/Gemini lightcurve: extreme 10:1 elongation (Meech et al., Nature)

- **kind:** `observation`

Meech et al. published the coordinated ESO VLT / Gemini / CFHT / UKIRT / Keck lightcurve campaign in Nature: brightness varied by a factor of ~10 every 7.3 hours, implying an axis ratio of at least ~10:1 (later refined to ~6:1–8:1), more elongated than any known Solar System asteroid or comet. Reddish surface, no dust, no coma, tumbling rotation. The 'cigar' (or 'pancake', per later modeling) shape became anomaly #1 in the alien-tech debate.

**Sources:** <https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso1737/> · <https://www.nature.com/articles/nature25020>

### 2017-12-13 — Breakthrough Listen scans 'Oumuamua with Green Bank: radio silence

- **kind:** `observation`

Breakthrough Listen pointed the 100-m Green Bank Telescope at 'Oumuamua for the first of four observing blocks (3:45–9:45 pm ET), sweeping 1–12 GHz across the L, S, C and X bands and accumulating 90 TB of raw data over the ~2-hour on-target observation. Sensitivity was good enough to detect a transmitter with the power of a cellphone at the object's distance in under a minute. No artificial signals were found then or in the completed analysis (Enriquez et al. 2018), the natural-vs-artificial question would instead be fought over dynamics, not radio.

**Sources:** <https://breakthroughinitiatives.org/news/14> · <https://breakthroughinitiatives.org/news/15> · <https://www.sci.news/astronomy/breakthrough-listen-no-artificial-signals-interstellar-asteroid-oumuamua-05550.html>

### 2018-06-27 — Non-gravitational acceleration detected (Micheli et al., Nature)

- **kind:** `observation`

Micheli et al. reported in Nature that 'Oumuamua's trajectory, reconstructed from ground data plus late Hubble astrometry through January 2018, required a non-gravitational acceleration of ~4.9e-6 m/s^2 at 1 AU, falling off roughly as 1/r^2. For a comet this is normal outgassing; for an object with no detected coma, dust, or tail it was a paradox. This single measurement is the load-bearing anomaly behind every exotic hypothesis that followed, from hydrogen icebergs to lightsails.

**Sources:** <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0254-4> · <https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso1820/>

### 2018-10-26 — Bialy & Loeb lightsail paper ignites global media storm

- **kind:** `statement`

Shmuel Bialy and Avi Loeb (Harvard CfA) posted 'Could Solar Radiation Pressure Explain 'Oumuamua's Peculiar Acceleration?' (arXiv:1810.11490, published ApJL 868 L1 on 2018-11-12). They showed radiation pressure could produce the observed acceleration if the object were a thin sheet with mass-to-area ratio ~0.1 g/cm^2, about 0.3–0.9 mm thick, a lightsail geometry, and floated that it 'may be a fully operational probe sent intentionally to Earth vicinity by an alien civilization.' The paper triggered a worldwide media storm and made Loeb the public face of the alien-artifact hypothesis.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11490> · <https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/2041-8213/aaeda8>

### 2018-11-14 — Spitzer non-detection caps the size and rules out normal outgassing

- **kind:** `observation`

Trilling et al. (AJ; NASA release Nov 14, 2018) reported that Spitzer stared at 'Oumuamua's position for 30.2 hours on 2017 Nov 21–22 and saw nothing in the infrared. The non-detection capped the size (effective radius roughly 49–220 m depending on albedo, implying a possibly highly reflective surface) and set tight limits on CO/CO2 outgassing, deepening the acceleration paradox: whatever pushed it was not detectable as gas or dust. Bialy & Loeb leaned on these limits.

**Sources:** <https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-learns-more-about-interstellar-visitor-oumuamua/> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08072>

### 2019-07-01 — ISSI team verdict: 'no compelling evidence to favor an alien explanation' (Nature Astronomy)

- **kind:** `statement`

The 14-member International Space Science Institute (ISSI) 'Oumuamua team ('The natural history of 'Oumuamua', Bannister et al., Nature Astronomy) published the mainstream consensus review: every observed property, shape, tumbling, color, acceleration, lies within plausible ranges for a natural interstellar planetesimal, and 'we find no compelling evidence to favor an alien explanation.' Loeb publicly rejected the conclusion; the paper remains the standard natural-origin reference against which his claims are measured.

**Sources:** <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-019-0816-x> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01910>

### 2020-06-09 — Seligman & Laughlin hydrogen-iceberg hypothesis (ApJL)

- **kind:** `statement`

Darryl Seligman and Gregory Laughlin proposed (ApJL 896 L8; arXiv:2005.12932) that 'Oumuamua was a hydrogen iceberg formed in the coldest cores of giant molecular clouds: H2 outgassing is nearly invisible (no dust, no detectable coma) yet provides the acceleration, and H2 sublimation sculpting naturally explains the extreme elongation. Critics (including Loeb, and Hoang & Loeb 2020) countered that H2 ice could not survive the interstellar journey, but the paper opened the 'exotic ice' family of natural explanations.

**Sources:** <https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/2041-8213/ab963f> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12932>

### 2021-01-26 — Loeb publishes 'Extraterrestrial': the case goes mainstream

- **kind:** `statement`

Avi Loeb's book 'Extraterrestrial: The First Sign of Intelligent Life Beyond Earth' (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt) hit shelves and became an international bestseller, arguing 'Oumuamua's anomalies, the acceleration without a coma, the extreme shape, the unusual initial rest-frame trajectory, are best explained as artificial technology, possibly a defunct lightsail. The book transformed a technical dispute into a public referendum on scientific open-mindedness and directly seeded Loeb's Galileo Project (announced July 2021).

**Sources:** <https://www.hmhbooks.com/shop/books/Extraterrestrial/9780358278146> · <https://avi-loeb.medium.com/>

### 2021-03-16 — Desch & Jackson: nitrogen-ice fragment of an exo-Pluto (JGR Planets)

- **kind:** `statement`

Steven Desch and Alan Jackson (ASU) published paired papers in JGR: Planets (AGU release Mar 16, 2021) proposing 'Oumuamua was a chip of solid nitrogen ice blasted off a Pluto-like exoplanet ~half a billion years ago: N2 sublimation matches the acceleration with no visible coma, and the high albedo of N2 ice allows a smaller body. Loeb and collaborators (Siraj & Loeb) rebutted that the required mass of exo-Pluto ejecta strains the galactic budget; Desch defended the numbers. The 'nitrogen iceberg' remains a leading natural candidate.

**Sources:** <https://news.agu.org/press-release/interstellar-object-oumuamua-may-be-chip-off-an-icy-n2-covered-exo-pluto/> · <https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2020JE006706>

### 2023-03-22 — Bergner & Seligman 'dark comet' H2 answer in Nature: and Loeb's rebuttal

- **kind:** `statement`

Jennifer Bergner (Berkeley) and Darryl Seligman published in Nature that cosmic-ray radiolysis of ordinary water ice during 'Oumuamua's interstellar transit created trapped molecular hydrogen; solar warming then released the H2, producing the acceleration with no visible coma, no exotic composition needed. Widely reported as 'mystery solved.' Within days Thiem Hoang and Avi Loeb posted a rebuttal (arXiv:2303.13861; Loeb's Medium essay ''Oumuamua Was Not a Hydrogen-Water Iceberg', Mar 2023) arguing that evaporative cooling by the escaping H2 leaves the surface a factor of ~9 colder than Bergner & Seligman assumed, cutting the H2 thrust below what is required; Bergner countered that the cooling effects do not change the conclusion. The dispute remains unresolved in print.

**Sources:** <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05687-w> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13861> · <https://avi-loeb.medium.com/oumuamua-was-not-a-hydrogen-water-iceberg-1dd2f7a6107f> · <https://www.centauri-dreams.org/2023/03/27/oumuamua-avi-loebs-response-to-the-molecular-hydrogen-theory/>

### 2025-08-06 — Loeb Scale retroactively ranks 'Oumuamua Level 4 (vs Borisov Level 0)

- **kind:** `statement`

Amid the 3I/ATLAS debate, Loeb formalized 'The Loeb Scale: Astronomical Classification of Interstellar Objects' (arXiv:2508.09167, submitted Aug 6, 2025; later published in the International Journal of Astrobiology), a 0-10 anomaly scale for interstellar objects modeled on the Torino Scale (Levels 0-3 natural, 4-7 increasingly anomalous, 8-10 confirmed artificial). Applied retroactively: 1I/'Oumuamua scores Level 4 (multiple unexplained anomalies), 2I/Borisov Level 0 (the perfectly natural control comet), and 3I/ATLAS Level 4. The scale is Loeb's institutionalization of his 1I-vs-2I framing: Borisov is what a normal interstellar comet looks like; 'Oumuamua is not that.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09167> · <https://avi-loeb.medium.com/the-loeb-scale-astronomical-classification-of-interstellar-objects-62b909644351> · <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-astrobiology/article/interstellar-object-significance-scale-loeb-scale-astronomical-classification-of-interstellar-objects/33F927C9600721DF14D1AA8525D7EDC0>

### 2026-07-24 — Outbound: 53.6 AU from the Sun, headed for Pegasus

- **kind:** `status`

Per JPL Horizons, 'Oumuamua is 53.58 AU from the Sun as of today (2026-07-24), beyond Pluto's average orbit, receding at ~27.0 km/s and asymptotically slowing toward its hyperbolic excess speed of 26.33 km/s, headed for the constellation Pegasus. It passed Neptune's orbital distance in 2022 and is far beyond recovery by any existing telescope; its true nature is now permanently unmeasurable unless a chase mission (e.g. a Project Lyra concept) is ever launched. Every remaining argument is fought over the 2017-2018 data alone.

**Sources:** <https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/ (COMMAND='1I', heliocentric r on 2026-07-24 = 53.580 AU, range-rate 27.0 km/s)> · <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/oumuamua/>

## 2I/Borisov — 14 events

### 2019-08-30 — Discovery by Gennadiy Borisov at MARGO, Crimea

- **kind:** `discovery`

Amateur astronomer Gennadiy Borisov discovers a ~magnitude 17.8 comet at low solar elongation using his self-built 0.65 m telescope at his MARGO observatory (MPC code L51) in Nauchnij, Crimea, the first interstellar object ever found by an amateur, and found while it was still inbound (~3 AU from the Sun), unlike 'Oumuamua which was found outbound. ### 2019-09-11 — Provisional designation C/2019 Q4 (Borisov); hyperbolic orbit suspected

- **kind:** `status`

The Minor Planet Center announces the object as comet C/2019 Q4 (Borisov) (MPEC 2019-R106), noting the orbit solution 'has converged to the hyperbolic elements shown below, which would indicate an interstellar origin.' The first published solution gave eccentricity e = 3.08, far beyond any bound solar-system orbit (later refined to e = 3.36), with JPL's Farnocchia, Bill Gray, and Dave Tholen independently reaching the same conclusion: a likely interstellar comet flagged within two weeks of discovery.

### 2019-09-20 — First gas detected: cyanogen (CN) with the William Herschel Telescope

Fitzsimmons et al. detect the CN (0-0) emission band at 388 nm using the 4.2 m William Herschel Telescope + ISIS spectrograph on La Palma (2019 Sep 20.2 UT), the first spectroscopic detection of gas from any interstellar object. CN production rate (3.7 +/- 0.4) x 10^24 molecules/s at r = 2.7 AU; follow-up work showed 2I is depleted in C2, but overall its gas chemistry looked like a normal solar-system comet (ApJL 885, L9; arXiv:1909.12144).

### 2019-09-24 — Confirmed interstellar: named 2I/Borisov (MPEC 2019-S72)

- **kind:** `status`

The IAU/Minor Planet Center issues MPEC 2019-S72, confirming the interstellar hyperbolic orbit (e = 3.372 in the circular's solution, from 472 observations spanning Aug 30 - Sep 24) and assigning the permanent designation 2I/Borisov, the second interstellar object and the first unambiguous interstellar comet. The IAU retained the discoverer's name, honoring the amateur discovery.

### 2019-10-12 — First Hubble imaging: tiny nucleus, radius under 0.5 km

HST/WFC3 obtains the sharpest images yet (Jewitt, Luu et al.), beginning a monitoring campaign through perihelion. Convolution modeling of the coma profile sets a nucleus radius limit of r <= 0.5 km (with r > 0.2 km from the non-gravitational acceleration constraint), roughly 15x smaller than early ground-based estimates (0.7-3.3 km), per NASA's release. ### 2019-12-08 — Perihelion at 2.006 AU

- **kind:** `close_approach`

2I/Borisov reaches perihelion at 2.006 AU (just outside Mars' orbit), moving ~44 km/s, well above the ~30 km/s local solar escape speed, with a hyperbolic excess velocity of ~32 km/s that guarantees it will leave the solar system. Throughout, it behaves as a classic active comet, sustained water and CO outgassing, dust coma and tail, the natural 'control case' against 'Oumuamua's inert, accelerating anomaly. Hubble imaged it near perihelion on Dec 9.

### 2019-12-28 — Closest approach to Earth: ~1.9 AU

- **kind:** `close_approach`

The comet makes its closest approach to Earth at roughly 1.9 AU (~290 million km), never a threat and never close, but well-placed for the VLT, ALMA, and Hubble campaigns that bracketed this date: ALMA had observed Dec 15-16, and Bagnulo's VLT/FORS2 polarimetric monitoring spanned late December 2019 through March 2020.

### 2020-03-09 — Double outburst: two brightenings in five days

Between March 4-9, 2020 (outbound, ~2.8 AU), Polish astronomers Drahus and Guzik (Jagiellonian University) with the OGLE team report the comet brightening ~0.7 magnitudes in two distinct outbursts within days, ~0.3 mag between Mar 4.3-5.3 and ~0.4 mag between Mar 8.3-9.3 UT (ATel #13549, issued Mar 12). ### 2020-03-30 — Hubble catches the nucleus splitting: transient double nucleus

- **kind:** `observation`

HST/WFC3 images from two programs (PI Jewitt, ATel #13611; PI Bolin, ATel #13613, announced early April 2020) reveal a fragment ~0.3 arcsec from the optocenter. A transient 'double nucleus' with ~0.6 km^2 cross-section (~10^5 kg of dust) appears on March 30, absent March 28 and gone by April 3. Jewitt, Kim & Mutchler interpret it as rotational bursting of meter-sized boulders ejected in the March outburst, not catastrophic disruption: the comet shed a splinter but survived (ApJL 896, L39).

**Sources:** <https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/2041-8213/ab99cb> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01242> · <https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ATel13613....1B/abstract> · <https://epss.ucla.edu/comet-borisov-the-first-confirmed-interstellar-comet-is-breaking-up/>

### 2020-04-20 — ALMA: unusually CO-rich coma (Cordiner et al., Nature Astronomy)

- **kind:** `observation`

Cordiner, Milam et al. publish ALMA observations (taken 2019 Dec 15-16) detecting HCN and CO, the first direct measurement of gas from another planetary system. CO is 9-26x more abundant relative to water than in average solar-system comets, suggesting 2I/Borisov formed in an extremely cold, CO-ice-rich outer region of its home system, perhaps around a carbon-rich or M-dwarf star (Nature Astronomy 4, 861; the chemically distinct fingerprint of an alien comet).

**Sources:** <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-020-1087-2> · <https://www.almaobservatory.org/en/press-releases/alma-reveals-unusual-composition-of-interstellar-comet-2i-borisov/> · <https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/04/200420124927.htm>

### 2021-03-30 — VLT polarimetry: 'the most pristine comet ever observed' (Bagnulo et al.)

- **kind:** `observation`

Bagnulo et al. publish VLT/FORS2 polarimetry (taken Dec 2019 - Mar 2020) in Nature Communications: 2I/Borisov's polarization exceeds that of all typical comets and its coma is polarimetrically homogeneous, resembling only the unique comet Hale-Bopp. Conclusion: it likely never passed close to any star before ours, possibly the first truly pristine comet ever observed, carrying unaltered material from its birth cloud (Nat. Commun. 12, 1797).

**Sources:** <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-22000-x> · <https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2106/> · <https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2021/03/210330121256.htm>

### 2021-05-19 — Atomic nickel vapor in a cold coma (Guzik & Drahus, Nature)

- **kind:** `observation`

Guzik & Drahus report gaseous atomic nickel in 2I/Borisov's coma at 2.322 AU (~180 K equilibrium temperature), produced at 0.9 +/- 0.3 x 10^22 atoms/s, likely from photolysis of a short-lived nickel-bearing molecule (Nature 593, 375). Heavy-metal vapor had previously been seen only in ultrahot environments like sungrazing comets. Published back-to-back with Manfroid et al.'s discovery of Fe and Ni vapor in ~20 solar-system comets, a shared, previously unnoticed chemistry linking 2I to home-grown comets and reinforcing its natural-comet status.

**Sources:** <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03485-4> · <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34012084/> · <https://www.sci.news/astronomy/nickel-interstellar-comet-2i-borisov-09675.html>

### 2025-08-06 — Loeb Scale ranks 2I/Borisov Level 0: the natural control case

- **kind:** `statement`

In the wake of 3I/ATLAS, the Interstellar Object Significance Scale ('Loeb Scale') paper by Omer Eldadi, Gershon Tenenbaum, and Avi Loeb (arXiv:2508.09167, submitted Aug 6, 2025; later Int. J. Astrobiology) retroactively scores the three known interstellar objects on its 0-10 scale: 1I/'Oumuamua Level 4 (anomalous), 3I/ATLAS Level 4, and 2I/Borisov Level 0, fully consistent with natural cometary behavior. Loeb has consistently treated Borisov as the benchmark 'ordinary comet' proving the detection pipeline works, sharpening the contrast with 'Oumuamua's anomalies.

**Sources:** <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09167> · <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-astrobiology/article/interstellar-object-significance-scale-loeb-scale-astronomical-classification-of-interstellar-objects/33F927C9600721DF14D1AA8525D7EDC0>

### 2026-07-24 — Outbound: receding toward interstellar space

- **kind:** `status`

2I/Borisov is far beyond observability, now tens of AU from the Sun and departing on its hyperbolic trajectory at ~32 km/s excess velocity, never to return. Its legacy: it confirmed that other planetary systems make comets much like ours (CN, HCN, dust, outbursts, nickel vapor), yet with distinct fingerprints (extreme CO enrichment, C2 depletion, unusually pristine dust), the well-behaved sibling that makes 'Oumuamua's and 3I/ATLAS's oddities stand out.

**Sources:** <https://theskylive.com/c2019q4-info> · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2I/Borisov> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00568>
