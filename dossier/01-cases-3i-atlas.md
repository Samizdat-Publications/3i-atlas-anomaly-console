### A-01 — Anomalously large size / interstellar mass budget

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-07-08
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Early photometry made 3I/ATLAS far brighter than expected for an interstellar object at ~4.5 AU: if the light came from a bare nucleus (albedo ~0.05), its radius would be ~10 km (~20 km diameter) — a mass Loeb put at roughly a million times 'Oumuamua's and a thousand times Borisov's (his July paper says over ten-million-fold and hundred-thousand-fold vs Borisov's core). Later HST/JWST work capped the nucleus far smaller (Jewitt et al. HST: radius ~0.22-2.8 km; Loeb's final essay uses a 2.6 km diameter). A separate September 2025 Loeb-Cloete-Veres analysis claimed the absence of pre-perihelion non-gravitational acceleration independently forced a mass above ~33 billion tons (diameter >5 km).

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb (arXiv:2507.05881, submitted 2025-07-08) argued the interstellar rocky-material reservoir cannot deliver ~20-km objects at this detection rate — one would expect millions of 'Oumuamua-sized objects for each object of 3I/ATLAS's mass scale, so it is either a comet with a small core (<0.6 km) or a member of an implausibly rare population exceeding the interstellar mass budget. In his November 2025 likelihood ranking he assigned the mass anomaly P<0.001, and he kept the 'mass budget discrepancy' as anomaly #1 of his final 22-item list, arguing planetary-disk reservoirs miss the required mass by orders of magnitude even for the revised 2.6-km diameter.

> [paraphrase] The inferred nucleus diameter of 2.6 kilometers and the number density of its parent population (assuming a natural comet) exceed the mass reservoir of planetary disks around low-metallicity stars by orders of magnitude.
>
> — https://avi-loeb.medium.com/3i-atlas-is-fading-away-leaving-us-to-ponder-over-its-22-mysterious-anomalies-8d1318565f13

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

The brightness is dominated by a dust coma, not the nucleus; Hubble imaging (Jewitt et al., Aug 2025, arXiv:2508.02934) constrained the nucleus radius to <2.8 km (albedo 0.04, lower bound ~0.22 km), eliminating the population problem. Kilometer-scale interstellar comets are consistent with expected ISO size distributions, and Rubin Observatory is expected to find many more. Jason Wright (AstroWright) notes Loeb's later 33-billion-ton mass floor rested on assuming non-gravitational-acceleration measurement precision of 1e-10 AU/day^2 when the actual precision is ~1e-7 — off by ~1000x.

**Verification notes:** Two fixes, rest confirmed. (1) CONFLATION: the "~33 billion tons" mass was wrongly paired with the July ~20-km bare-nucleus inference. 33 billion tons (3.3e13 kg, with diameter >5 km) is the lower bound from a separate September 2025 Loeb-Cloete-Veres claim that 3I/ATLAS showed no pre-perihelion non-gravitational acceleration — the very claim Jason Wright showed rested on assuming measurement precision of 1e-10 AU/day^2 when actual precision is ~1e-7 (off ~1000x). The July 8 arXiv paper (2507.05881) instead argued a ~10 km radius (albedo 0.05) implying mass "over ten million times" 'Oumuamua's and "at least a hundred thousand times" Borisov's core (publicly framed by Loeb as "a million times 'Oumuamua, a thousand times Borisov"), and that "one would expect millions of objects like 'Oumuamua for each object on the mass scale of 3I/ATLAS." (2) MISATTRIBUTION: P<0.001 was not assigned in the July arXiv paper; Loeb assigned P<0.001 to the mass anomaly in his November 2025 Medium essay "Anomalies of 3I/ATLAS, Organized by Likelihood." Confirmed: date 2025-07-08 matches arXiv:2507.05881 v1; Jewitt et al. HST (arXiv:2508.02934, Aug 2025) nucleus radius <2.8 km (albedo 0.04), lower bound ~0.22 km if CO-driven; Loeb's final 22-anomaly essay lists the mass-budget discrepancy as anomaly #1 using a 2.6-km nucleus diameter and "orders of magnitude" reservoir shortfall; official explanation (dust-coma-dominated brightness, km-scale nucleus consistent with ISO size distribution, Rubin expected to find more) is fairly stated. ### A-02 — Ecliptic-plane alignment (~5 deg, 0.2%)

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-07-16
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`
- **Loeb Scale (third-party rank):** 4

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

3I/ATLAS's retrograde hyperbolic trajectory (inclination ~175 deg) lies within about 5 degrees of the ecliptic — the orbital plane of the planets. For a randomly oriented incoming interstellar trajectory, Loeb computed a ~0.2% chance of such alignment.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

In the July 2025 paper 'Is the Interstellar Object 3I/ATLAS Alien Technology?' (Hibberd, Crowl, Loeb; arXiv v1 submitted July 16, 2025), this was the founding anomaly: a low retrograde tilt to the ecliptic 'offers various benefits to an Extra-terrestrial Intelligence,' making the path look like a designed reconnaissance trajectory with optimal access to the inner planets. After introducing the Loeb scale in August 2025 (arXiv:2508.09167), Loeb kept this among the core set anchoring his rating — 'there are eight anomalies that endow it with a rank of 4 on the Loeb scale.'

> aligned to within 5 degrees with the orbital plane of the planets
>
> — '3I/ATLAS is Fading Away, Leaving Us to Ponder Over Its 22 Mysterious Anomalies', Medium, March 12, 2026

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Mainstream astronomers call this a textbook a-posteriori probability fallacy: the specific property was chosen after observing it ('he chose specific aspects of the comet's orbit to compute probabilities for after he knew what they were' — Jason Wright), and with any object some ~0.2%-level coincidences are guaranteed among the many orbital parameters one could examine. AstroWright (Nov 9, 2025) labels it a 'classic fallacy and misuse of probability theory,' citing Hector Socas-Navarro's detailed critique; there is also mild survey selection bias toward ecliptic discoveries. Dynamically the orbit is unremarkable for a galactic-disk object.

Wright). Two fixes: (1) official_explanation quoted Wright as 'classic misuse of probability theory' — his exact wording is 'classic fallacy and misuse of probability theory'; corrected to exact quote and added his a-posteriori elaboration. (2) loeb_take implied the rank-of-4 / '8 anomalies' statement came from the July 2025 paper; the Loeb scale was actually introduced in his August 2025 paper (arXiv:2508.09167) and the verified sentence 'there are eight anomalies that endow it with a rank of 4 on the Loeb scale' comes from later Medium posts. ### A-03 — Fine-tuned arrival geometry (Mars/Venus/Jupiter passes)

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-07-16
- **Verification:** `CONFIRMED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

The arrival time placed 3I/ATLAS within tens of millions of km of three planets: ~29 million km (0.19 AU) from Mars on Oct 3, 2025, ~0.65 AU from Venus in November, and 53.6 million km (0.358 AU) from Jupiter on Mar 16, 2026. Loeb estimated a combined probability of order 0.005% (he later listed P=0.00005) for a random interstellar object to thread this sequence.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

In the July 2025 paper and later essays, Loeb argued the timing looks 'fine-tuned' for planetary reconnaissance — exactly the flyby program a probe designer would choose. This was one of the original 8 anomalies behind his rank-4 assignment and remained #3 in his final 22-item list.

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Astronomers respond that any object traveling in the ecliptic plane will necessarily pass 'near' planets, so the claim double-counts the ecliptic-alignment anomaly; AstroWright calls it question-begging, noting Loeb treats both close approaches and non-close approaches as anomalous. Tens of millions of km are distant passes with no operational significance. NASA states all evidence points to a natural comet.

### A-04 — Perihelion hidden behind the Sun

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-07-16
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Perihelion occurred Oct 29, 2025 (Oct 29-30 across time zones) at 1.356 AU while 3I/ATLAS was on the far side of the Sun as seen from Earth (solar conjunction Oct 21) — unobservable by ground-based telescopes from late September until it re-emerged in early December 2025.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

In the July 16, 2025 arXiv paper (Hibberd, Crowl & Loeb, arXiv:2507.12213), Loeb and coauthors flagged that this geometry would let a technological probe execute a clandestine reverse Solar Oberth maneuver — an optimal braking burn at maximum solar depth — while hidden from Earth. In his ranked anomaly lists this is folded into the 'fine-tuned arrival time' anomaly (P=0.00005, Nov 23, 2025 Medium list), and he explicitly called for post-conjunction astrometry to check for maneuvers.

> The eclipse by the Sun from Earth of 3I/ATLAS at perihelion, would allow it to conduct a clandestine reverse Solar Oberth Manoeuvre, an optimal high-thrust strategy for interstellar spacecraft to brake and stay bound to the Sun.
>
> — Hibberd, Crowl & Loeb, 'Is the Interstellar Object 3I/ATLAS Alien Technology?', arXiv:2507.12213 (submitted Jul 16, 2025)

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Perihelion viewing geometry is set by chance orbital phase; solar conjunctions like this are routine for comets. Moreover the object was NOT unobserved: STEREO (Sep 11-Oct 2), SOHO/LASCO (Oct 16-26), GOES-19 CCOR-1, PUNCH, and Parker Solar Probe's WISPR (Oct 15-Nov 5) imaged it through conjunction and perihelion, Mars orbiters (MRO/HiRISE, ExoMars TGO) imaged it at the ~Oct 3 Mars flyby, and Europa Clipper observed it Nov 6. It re-emerged on a trajectory consistent with a natural comet; the small (~3.7-sigma) non-gravitational acceleration reported by JPL/MPC is attributed by most astronomers to ordinary cometary outgassing.

### A-05 — Sunward anti-tail / collimated sunward jet

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-08-05
- **Verification:** `CONFIRMED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

The Hubble image of July 21, 2025 showed the dust distribution extended toward the Sun, about ten times longer than wide, with no classical dust tail; a sunward anti-tail re-appeared in July-August and again in November 2025, eventually reaching ~1 million km. Mars HiRISE imaging on Oct 2, 2025 also showed a glowing extension ahead of the object along its direction of motion.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb argued this is a genuine sunward-pointing jet, not the perspective illusion seen in thousands of normal comets (P<0.001 in his Nov 23, 2025 likelihood-ordered list), and that sustaining a collimated sunward plume is more naturally explained by a thruster pointed at the Sun for braking than by sublimating ice.

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Anti-tails and sunward fans are well-documented comet phenomena; Jewitt's HST modeling explains the geometry with large, slow-moving dust grains ejected from the sunlit hemisphere that lag the nucleus, combined with projection effects. AstroWright notes the underlying mechanism has been understood for ~50 years and similar features appear in comets like 17P/Holmes and C/2016 R2.

### A-06 — CO2-dominated coma; water only ~4% of plume

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-08-25
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

JWST NIRSpec observations of Aug 6, 2025 at 3.32 AU (Cordiner et al., arXiv:2508.18209) found a carbon-dioxide-dominated gas coma with CO2/H2O = 7.6 +/- 0.3 (~8:1) — among the highest ratios ever observed in any comet, ~4.5 sigma above the trend for solar-system comets (survey median ~0.12). By mass the plume was ~87% CO2, ~9% CO, and only ~4% water.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb listed the near-absence of water — the defining volatile of familiar comets — as a minor anomaly at P<0.1 in his 13-item likelihood-ranked list (Nov 23, 2025), suggesting the 'gas plume' might not be cometary sublimation at all but sunlight releasing ices and dust accumulated on the surface of a technological object during its interstellar journey. It is not a separate item in his final 22-anomaly list (Mar 12, 2026), compiled after post-perihelion water detections.

> The gas plume surrounding 3I/ATLAS contains only 4% water by mass, a dominant constituent of familiar solar-system comets.
>
> — https://avi-loeb.medium.com/anomalies-of-3i-atlas-organized-by-likelihood-af20fb3b6d21

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

A CO2-rich nucleus is natural for a body formed near its parent disk's CO2 snowline or processed by galactic cosmic rays over billions of years (arXiv:2510.26308); at >3 AU water sublimates inefficiently while CO2 vents freely. Water/OH production rose strongly closer to the Sun — MeerKAT detected 1665/1667 MHz OH absorption on Oct 24, 2025 (ATel #17473), and SOHO/SWAN measured ~3.2e29 water molecules/s near perihelion — and interstellar comets are expected to show compositional diversity.

### A-07 — Nickel emission without iron, and nickel far above the coma's ordinary gas

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-08-28
- **Verification:** `CORRECTED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

VLT/UVES spectroscopy (early Jul-Aug 21, 2025; ~4.4 to 2.85 AU inbound) detected strong atomic nickel (Ni I) emission with iron (Fe I) undetected — by Aug 21 the Ni production rate was 4.6 +/- 0.7 g/s versus CN at 17.6 +/- 2.0 g/s, rising steeply (Q(Ni) proportional to r^-7.7). Keck II/KCWI imaging (Aug 24, published Oct 2025) confirmed the Ni is concentrated within ~600 km of the nucleus (e-folding radius 593.7 +/- 14.8 km vs 841 km for CN). The Ni/CN ratio (~0.1) is orders of magnitude above the solar-system comet median and higher than 2I/Borisov.

**CLAIM — the anomaly / technosignature reading**

Loeb stressed that release of nickel without iron matches the industrial nickel-refining process via nickel tetracarbonyl, Ni(CO)4 — writing that nickel without iron 'is a signature of industrial production of nickel alloys' and that the carbonyl channel is 'an extremely rare and exotic possibility in comets, whereas it is a standard technology for industrial nickel refining.' He treats the nickel-to-cyanide ratio as a second and separate count against a natural reading: at roughly 0.1 it sits orders of magnitude above the median for solar-system comets and above 2I/Borisov, the only other interstellar comet with comparable measurements. The argument is that the absence of iron says something about the MECHANISM, while the Ni/CN ratio says something about the AMOUNT — that whatever is releasing this nickel is releasing far more of it than ordinary cometary chemistry produces, relative to the gas the coma is otherwise made of.

> is a signature of industrial production of nickel alloys
>
> — Avi Loeb, 'A Steeply-Rising Production of Cyanide and Nickel Without Iron in the Gas Plume Around 3I/ATLAS', Medium, Aug 28, 2025 (verified)

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

Ni and Fe emission had already been detected together (at roughly equal abundance) in ~20 solar-system comets and in 2I/Borisov (Manfroid et al. 2021), with low-temperature carbonyl/metal-organic sublimation a leading natural mechanism. For 3I/ATLAS, the observing teams themselves proposed natural in-situ Ni(CO)4 (or Ni-organic/PAH complex) formation and photodissociation near the nucleus — which predicts exactly the observed central concentration — with Fe carbonyl being less volatile and Fe I lines harder to excite/detect at large distances. Fe was subsequently detected as 3I/ATLAS approached the Sun (arXiv 2509.26053), and the Ni/Fe ratio dropped rapidly with decreasing heliocentric distance, trending toward normal comet values. Astronomers (e.g. Jason Wright) call it an extreme but chemically explicable value, not evidence of artificial nickel plating. THE RATIO ARGUMENT HAS A DIFFERENT ANSWER FROM THE IRON ONE, and it turns on the denominator. Ni/CN can be large because there is a great deal of nickel or because there is very little CN, and 3I/ATLAS is independently known to be poor in exactly the gas species that sit in the denominator: its coma is CO2-dominated, with water at only about 4% of the plume, measured over the same inbound stretch. A ratio taken against a depleted denominator is not the same claim as an excess in the numerator, and nothing in these spectra separates the two. Two further features of the measurement cut the same way. The Ni and CN emission do not come from the same volume — nickel is concentrated within an e-folding radius of 593.7 +/- 14.8 km of the nucleus while CN extends to 841 km — so the ratio compares two differently distributed species and partly reports where each is produced rather than how much of each exists. And the whole measurement is heliocentric-distance dependent: these spectra were taken from 4.4 down to 2.85 AU, and the Ni/Fe ratio that looked equally extreme over that same stretch fell rapidly toward ordinary comet values as the object approached the Sun. The extreme numbers live in the far-out regime for both ratios, which is what a volatility-and-excitation effect looks like and is not what a bulk composition looks like. The honest position is that the Ni/CN value is genuinely at the edge of the measured distribution and that the catalog of comets measured this way at these distances is small.

### A-26 — Extreme methanol enrichment (CH3OH/HCN) measured by ALMA

- **Object:** 3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1)
- **Date:** 2025-09-15
- **Verification:** `CONFIRMED`

**OBSERVATION — what was measured**

Roth, Cordiner and colleagues used the Atacama Compact Array of ALMA to detect methanol toward 3I/ATLAS on 2025 August 28, September 18 and 22, and October 1, and hydrogen cyanide on September 12 and 15 — pre-perihelion, at heliocentric distances from 2.6 down to 1.7 AU. Three results came out of it. The two molecules outgassed DIFFERENTLY: HCN production was depleted in the sunward hemisphere of the coma while methanol was enhanced in that same direction. Methanol showed production from coma sources beyond 258 km from the nucleus at 99% confidence, consistent with release from icy grains behaving as miniature comets, though low signal-to-noise on the long baselines that probe the smallest scales stopped the authors from ruling out methanol as a purely nucleus-derived species. HCN, by contrast, was indistinguishable from direct sublimation off the nucleus. And the methanol production rate rose sharply from August through October, with an uptick near the inner edge of the water sublimation zone at 2 AU. The headline number is the ratio: CH3OH/HCN of 124 (+30/-34) on September 12 and 79 (+11/-14) on September 15 — among the most methanol-enriched values measured in any comet at radio wavelengths.

>

**COUNTER-READING — the mainstream explanation**

The measurement is real, was published in a refereed letter, and the enrichment is genuinely extreme. What it is not is unprecedented — and the paper says so itself in the same sentence. The one object that beats 3I/ATLAS on this measure is C/2016 R2 (PanSTARRS), a comet that formed in OUR solar system. If a native comet can be more methanol-enriched than the interstellar visitor, then this ratio cannot by itself mark an object as foreign, let alone as manufactured. Three further points come straight from the data. First, CH3OH/HCN is a RATIO, and it rises just as readily when HCN is scarce as when methanol is abundant — 3I/ATLAS was already known to be depleted in nitrogen-bearing volatiles, which lifts the quotient without requiring anything unusual in the numerator. Second, the two values are one noisy quantity measured three days apart: 124 (+30/-34) and 79 (+11/-14) overlap inside their own error bars, so quoting them as a span from 70 to 120 reports the scatter as if it were a trend. Third, the extended methanol source is textbook cometary physics rather than an exhaust plume — icy grains lifted into the coma, warmed by sunlight, releasing gas as they go, which is exactly why methanol appears enhanced on the sunward side where the grains are hottest, while HCN coming straight off the nucleus does not. The sharp rise from August to October tracks the inner edge of the water sublimation zone at 2 AU, which is where a sublimation-driven increase is supposed to happen. The honest residue is that this composition is rare and interesting: an object mixed in a different environment from ours, carrying a volatile inventory we have essentially one solar-system analogue for. That is a strong statement about where it formed. It is not evidence of a propellant tank.
